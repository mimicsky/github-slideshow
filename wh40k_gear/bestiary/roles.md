# Enemy roles: personalities and tendencies

How each enemy role tends to think and fight. **These are tendencies, not scripts:**
the DM decides what any enemy actually does, and a frightened sniper or a clever
brute is always allowed. Rules in **bold** are the role's mechanics.

**How side notes work: they are add-ons, not replacements.** Each role's main
entry is how *every* enemy of that role behaves. A side note (for cultists,
traitor elites, raiders and so on) adds to it for that enemy type. A cultist
sniper is the basic sniper **plus** the cultist note. Only where a note speaks to
the same point does it change that one point (the basic sniper moves after two
shots; a cultist sniper doesn't move); everything else in the main entry still
applies.

## Grunt (ranged)

**Who has it:** all five types (a grunt is anyone who rolls 1-14).

The rank and file. Wants to live through this. Shoots from cover, follows whoever
is shouting orders, ducks when shot at, and is the first to run when the leader
falls (unless an enforcer is watching). Fights better in numbers; alone, often
surrenders.

*Side note: cultists and traitor elites are braver: they hold longer when the leader
falls and rarely surrender (fanatics and fallen professionals).*

## Grunt (melee)

**Who has it:** all five types (a grunt is anyone who rolls 1-14).

The ones who couldn't get a gun, or prefer not to. Rushes when the group rushes,
hangs back otherwise. Waits behind cover for the enemy to come close, **moves from
cover to cover** to close the gap, and **rushes anyone caught out of place**: alone,
in the open, reloading. Brave in a crowd, nervous alone.

*Side note: cultists and raiders are braver: they don't care about being alone and
will run straight into danger.*

*Side note: traitor elites are smarter: they wait in cover for their team to
suppress, then rush; they watch for a reload or a vent, find gaps, and jump cover
to cover far better than the rest.*

## Sniper

**Who has it:** all five types.

Patient and cold, and **harder to spot**. Picks a high or distant spot, waits for a
shot, and aims for the important people on a team and anyone exposed. **Moves after
two shots.** Doesn't fight up close: runs if rushed, and will most often surrender
if cornered or reached.

*Side note: cultist snipers stay in one spot no matter how many shots they take,
shoot whoever they see with no care who it is, won't run, and will take a close
fight.*

*Side note: traitor elite snipers get **+1 to shooting**, wait longer for a shot,
move quickly after every shot, play behind their team so they can't be rushed, and
will run but will take a close fight.*

## Grenadier

**Who has it:** all five types.

Likes clusters. Thinks in **three ways**: waits for a team to bunch up and lobs into
the cluster; throws to **flush people out of cover** for the shooters; and goes for
a target caught in a **cramped space**. Throws into doorways before anyone goes
through.

*Side note: cultist and raider grenadiers don't think, they just lob. Careless:
they throw even when it puts their own team in danger, and they throw several
grenades into the same spot.*

## Heavy gunner

**Who has it:** all five types.

One soldier with a heavy gun. Sets up somewhere solid and hoses the lane the party
has to cross. **Suppresses: anyone they're firing at takes -2 on their shots.** Slow
to move and slow to turn; **flank them or pressure them back** and they struggle.

*Side note: cultist and raider heavy gunners are fearless: they walk toward you
while firing, even if it overheats the gun, and care about emptying the mag more
than anything. **Their suppression is -3** (instead of -2), but **they take -2 on
their own shots** from firing on the move.*

*Side note: traitor elite heavy gunners suppress from cover and reload before they
run dry, move from cover to cover while firing, and if they run out of ammo they
switch to a backup weapon or take ammo from the ground or a teammate. Pressure
doesn't bother them.*

## Heavy team

**Who has it:** raiders, pirates, traitor soldiers, traitor elites.

Two people, gunner and loader, on a big gun. Takes a strong position early and holds
it. **One roll, two bodies: it takes two kills to silence it.** **Kill one and the gun
fires only every other beat** (the survivor works it alone), and there's a **50%
chance the survivor runs**.

*Side note: cultist heavy teams never run; the survivor keeps working the gun.*

*Side note: traitor elite heavy teams never run either: if one goes down, the
survivor always takes cover and fights on with their own gun or one off the
ground.*

## Plasma / melta gunner

**Who has it:** raiders, pirates, traitor soldiers, traitor elites.

Looks for a big shot on a **priority target**: armour, a door, a vehicle, whoever
looks most important or dangerous. Careful about heat; will vent rather than risk
it unless desperate. **Prone to surrendering if the gun is overheated and they have
no backup weapon.**

*Side note: cultist plasma gunners don't care about priorities or heat, and won't
surrender.*

*Side note: traitor soldier plasma gunners are very careful about heat, but get
careless with it when pressured, and will surrender most of the time when
overheated, even with a backup weapon.*

## Flamer

**Who has it:** all five types.

Wants to be close and loves close quarters. Clears cover by burning it, and **sprays
fire over cover to suppress** whoever is behind it. Moves forward behind other
troops. **The tank is a target**: a solid hit on it can make it burst. **On a burst,
roll a d6: 1-4, the flamer (the user) goes up in a ball of flame, and so does the
floor around them: they **spend 2 turns putting themselves out**; 5-6, the weapon goes up in flames and they lose their next turn.**

*Side note: cultist flamers only like close range: they only take fights there,
rush to get there, and will burn their own melee fighters if they're in the way.*

*Side note: traitor soldier and traitor elite flamers burn doors and exits so you
can't leave, and always suppress cover when their side pushes or rushes. On the
burst d6, a 6 means they throw the burning gun away instead, and don't lose the
turn.*

## Demolitions

**Who has it:** all five types.

Mines the approach, rigs doors, blows cover out from under the party. **Their traps
can be spotted with a Perception check.** Prefers to fight before the fight: traps
set in advance. Will blow something in a panic rather than be taken. **If surprised
before they can set up, they're more prone to surrender once they start losing.**

*Side note: cultist demolitions rig the whole area, but their traps are easier to
spot. They rig themselves too as a last resort: they won't surrender, and will
run into you and blow themselves up.*

## Medic

**Who has it:** all five types.

Stays behind the line and drags the wounded back. **Patches one ally a turn: moves
them back one health state, once per person** (Down to Hurt, Hurt to Fine); **can't
bring back the dead.** Shoots until someone on their side gets hurt, then switches to
patching. **Killing the medic hurts the team's morale.**

*Side note: cultist medics don't heal the normal way; they mostly use drugs, and
only heal someone who's Down.*

*Side note: raider medics use smoke to cover dragging and healing, and only heal
someone who's Down.*

## Chem-doctor

**Who has it:** cultists, raiders, pirates.

The medic's ugly cousin. **Doses allies with combat drugs at the start of the fight
and again mid-fight** (+2 to one ally for the fight, with the usual backfire).
Patches wounds with stimms (and, for cultists, their god's gifts), and carries
heretic cures, tonics and charms the Inquisition would burn. **If their side starts
losing badly, they overdose on every drug they have left and go into a rage:** each
dose gives +2 in melee and they ignore Hurt, each dose rolls its own backfire, and
when the rage ends they collapse. Cruel and curious; likes to experiment on
prisoners. **Their bag is loot, and a crime to keep.**

## Leader

**Who has it:** all five types.

The voice of the group: preacher, boss, captain, sergeant or officer. Stays where
they can see and be heard, **gives orders (+1 to their side's rolls)**, and keeps the
rest fighting. Usually fights hard when cornered. **Kill or silence them and the
rest waver** (grunts may run or surrender).

*Side note: cultist leaders chant and inspire: their side is braver, and
**suppression on them is 1 less**. When the leader goes down, the whole group becomes
more likely to surrender, even fanatics.*

*Side note: pirate leaders fight up close with the melee fighters and make them
better (**+1 in melee**). Killing them has no effect on the rest.*

*Side note: traitor elite leaders call out tactics and strategy, fighting from
cover further back and taking the odd shot. They keep giving orders even when
Down. Killing them has little effect.*

## Bodyguard

**Who has it:** all five types.

Shield, armour, and loyalty. **Sticks to the leader; with no leader, guards the most valuable specialist** (the witch, the heavy, the vox man). **Once a beat, can take a hit aimed at the one they guard.** Doesn't chase; stays put.

*Side note: cultist and raider bodyguards throw themselves onto any danger (a bomb, a grenade, a heavy-hitting shot) to block it. If the hit kills them, their body soaks all of it and no one else takes damage. If they survive it, the hit carries on to the others as normal.*

*Side note: traitor elite bodyguards peel for their team, charge in front for melee, and back up in front of a retreating team. They let the one they guard use them as movable cover, moving with them as they move.*

## Enforcer

**Who has it:** traitor soldiers, traitor elites.

A renegade commissar or the boss's executioner. Watches their own side more than
the party. **Stays in cover, mostly at the back.** **While the enforcer stands,
the group doesn't break or surrender**; they shoot the first one who runs. Hated
by their own men. **Killing one of their own takes the enforcer's turn, then roll
a d20 for the group: on 1-4, someone in the group turns and shoots the enforcer.**

## Icon bearer

**Who has it:** all five types.

Carries the banner, idol or colours. Stays near the middle where it can be seen.
**Allies within sight of the icon get +1 to rolls.** Fanatical about it; won't
drop it willingly. **Can plant the banner to keep it up.** **Dropping the banner
or destroying it is the only way to remove its buffs.**

*Side note: cultists are fanatical about their banners. Destroying it can send
them into a panic: -1 to rolls. They try to save it while it's being destroyed,
and try to pick it up if it has fallen.*

*Side note: raiders are prouder of who they fight for: the icon gives them +2 to
rolls instead of +1. Destroying or dropping their banner makes them angry, and
their basic grunts, melee and ranged, charge.*

## Rogue psyker

**Who has it:** all five types.

Unsanctioned and dangerous, mostly to themselves. Uses powers when they feel safe
or desperate. **Perils trigger 1-4** (see Perils of the Warp in the Codex, Gear rules).
Other enemies keep their distance. Their own team sees them as the most dangerous
thing on the field, to them and to the enemy.

*Side note: cultist psykers are blessed by their god with special powers. Their
powers hit harder (+2 to power attacks), but the warp gets in easier: **Perils
trigger 1-6**. Their own side never shoots them, no matter what; that would disgrace
their god.*

*Side note: pirate and raider psykers are ruthless. They push the warp harder and
harder and don't care what it does to them. If they're pressured too much or
their team starts to lose, they panic and push it more: -1 to their Perils trigger rolls.*

## Vox / auspex

**Who has it:** all five types.

Carries a vox, an auspex, or both: the eyes and ears. Calls in help, warns the
others, stays behind cover more, and only fights when they need to. **Kill them
first, or more enemies arrive.**

- **Vox:** can hack into the party's comms and listen in, but can't cut them off.
  If the party talks on comms before the fight, the enemy may already know.
- **Auspex:** the group **can't be ambushed** while they're on the team. Once the
  action starts, they fight as normal.

*Side note: cultist and raider vox/auspex care more about fighting than calling
in support.*

*Side note: pirate and traitor soldier vox/auspex care more about
calling for reinforcements than fighting. Once their team starts fighting, they
fall back to make the call.*

## Tech-adept / heretek

**Who has it:** all five types.

Fixes things mid-fight: clears jams, restarts a dead turret, re-arms a mine. Sets
up an auto-turret or locks a door the party needs. **Each of these costs 1 beat.**
A heretek may **jam the party's vox, making it unusable** (they cut it off but
learn nothing from it). When their own side retreats, they make it harder for the
party to follow. Protected by the others when possible.

## Breacher

**Who has it:** all five types.

Goes through doors first. Shields, shotguns, SMGs, always ready; rushes straight
into the room. Loves tight spaces: **+1 to shooting in close quarters.** Out in
the open, much less dangerous: **-1 to shooting.**

*Side note: cultist breachers rush with extreme speed (+2 DEX). They only like
close quarters; out in the open they stay in cover unless you come to them.*

*Side note: traitor soldier and traitor elite breachers pop from cover to cover
taking pot shots at you, then rush in with melee.*

## Assassin

**Who has it:** cultists, raiders, pirates, traitor elites.

**Proficient with knives and pistols: +2 to hit with them** (they need a lower
roll to land). Patient and quiet. **Hides anywhere they can fit or climb:** in
crates, on the walls, under the floor, in the ceiling. Picks one target and
**waits until they're alone, Down, or cornered.** Goes for the leader, the medic
or the best fighter, and will even wait until their whole team is dead to get the
drop. Uses poison and silence. **Avoids a fair fight: if outnumbered, runs and
hides.** Loves a 1v1.

*Side note: cultist assassins only wait until their team has the advantage, or
until their whole team is dead. The longer they wait, the more impatient they get.*

*Side note: traitor elite assassins are the most patient. They wait until the
fight is well over, and longer. They won't attack unless they know it's the
perfect moment and it's in their favour. Much harder to spot, with much cleverer
hiding spots.*

## Infiltrator

**Who has it:** raiders, traitor soldiers, traitor elites.

**Starts hidden** behind or beside the party, already in position. **Never moves
with their team; always lags behind it.** If the party spots the team from
hiding, the infiltrator won't be with them. **Light gear and quiet guns only**:
silenced pistols, suppressed rifles, knives, cloaks. Stays hidden until the
moment is best, then **ambushes from the rear**. Fades back if spotted.

*Side note: raider infiltrators carry grenades and ambush the ambushers. If the
party spots the enemy and sets up an ambush, the infiltrator comes out and hits
the party first.*

## Gunslinger

**Who has it:** raiders, pirates.

Cocky, flashy and very good. **Rolls 50/50 for two pistols or one good gun.**
**+3 on shooting.** **Seeks a one-on-one with the party's best shooter**, even if
it costs their team: calls them out, steps into the open to duel. Talks the whole
time. Pride is their weakness: they can be baited.

## Brute / mutant

**Who has it:** cultists, raiders, traitor soldiers, traitor elites.

Huge: a mutant, a renegade Ogryn, a penal monster. **Takes three times the hits to
drop.** Slow-witted, straightforward: walks at the nearest enemy and hits them.
Smashes cover. Can be led into traps, and sometimes ignores orders.

## Beast handler

**Who has it:** all five types.

Comes with **2-3 beasts** (hounds, mutant dogs, cyber-mastiffs, warp-hounds) that
fight on their own. Sends them first to run the party down and pin the wounded.
Kill the handler and the beasts may scatter, or go wild.

## Berserker

**Who has it:** all five types.

Drugged out of their mind and wants to kill something close. Charges the nearest
target, ignores orders and cover. **Doses: +2 in melee and ignores Hurt; with
doses left, keeps fighting a beat after going Down.**

## Bomb zealot

**Who has it:** cultists.

A cultist who wants to die well. Runs straight at the party (or a vehicle, or a
door) with explosives strapped on. **If they reach their target, they go off.**
Shoot them before they arrive; a solid hit on the charges may set them off early,
where they stand.

## Blessed

**Who has it:** cultists only.

A cultist touched by their god (catalogue, Heretic and cult items, Blessed
cultists). Fanatical, unafraid, often the one others rally around. Their god's
blessing shapes how they fight: Khorne charges, Nurgle walks through fire,
Slaanesh duels, Tzeentch plans.
