# Heretic and cult items

Item rules for this category. Things only heretics should own. Carrying them is a
crime; the Inquisition (and Kesh) will want every one.

## Chaos grimoire

(rare, 12 TV on the black market; player design.) A cultist's forbidden book:
hand-bound, stitched skin or cracked leather, pages in blood, chalk-ink or a
script that won't hold still. **Mostly the DM's call.** Most are a madman's
scribble. Some are real, and those are dangerous.

**Opening or reading it:** the DM rolls a d20 **in secret** and decides what it
means, using this as a guide:

| d20 | Roughly |
| --- | --- |
| 1-5 | **Something very bad.** A warp-whisper that won't leave (WIS save or the DM gives a lasting quirk or nightmare), a possession attempt, lights dying and frost, something on the other side noticing the reader, or a ward that bursts and burns |
| 6-12 | **Nothing.** Ravings, recipes, lists of names and filth |
| 13-17 | **A clue.** A cult location, a name, a date, a ritual's purpose, a link to another thread (Velk, Thask, the gardeners) |
| 18-20 | **Real knowledge, at a price.** A rite, a true name or a warding; using it costs something the DM chooses, now or later |

- A **purity seal** gives advantage on any save it forces, and burns away doing it.
- A **psyker** feels it before it's opened.
- **Destroying** one properly takes fire and a priest's rite (or a blessed flamer);
  tearing pages out doesn't count, and it may not want to burn.
- **Handing it to the Inquisition** is the safe choice and earns favour. Keeping one
  is heresy if anyone finds out.
- Enemy cult leaders and witches may carry one (found-or-made, rare).

### In a cultist's hands: the chant

A cultist with a **real** grimoire (the DM decides) can use it as a weapon. They
start **chanting** from it, and everyone nearby hears and sees it: the words are
wrong, the air thickens, lights flicker. The chant builds over beats, and the
**Light gets a chance to stop it** before it lands.

- **Breaking the chant:** wounding the chanter, knocking the book away, a flash
  or stun, or anything that makes them stop for a beat ends it. It starts over
  from nothing.
- **Each beat of chanting, the DM picks one effect** (or lets it build to the next):

| Beats chanted | What it can do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **A curse on one target in sight:** WIS save or -2 on everything next beat; or a lash of warp-force that hits like a lasgun and ignores flak |
| 2 | **Fear or fury:** everyone of the Light within ~10 m makes a WIS save or freezes for a beat; or every cultist nearby gets +1 next beat; or warp-fire on one target (hits like a flamer) |
| 3 | **The big one, DM's choice:** a small daemon or warp-thing torn through, a warp storm in the room (lights out, frost, everyone at -2), the god's gift on the chanter (healed, mutated, stronger), or something the cult's god would do |

- **Backlash:** each beat the chanter rolls a d20; on **1-3** the warp bites them
  instead (they drop a health step, and on a natural 1 something worse, DM's call).
  The book doesn't care who it eats.
- A **purity seal** gives advantage on saves against the chant, and burns away.
- A **psyker** in the Light can try to fight the chant (opposed WIS roll; win and
  it breaks).

## Chaos psyker staff

(rare, 25 TV on the black market; player design.) A cultist witch's focus: a
staff of black iron, bone or warped wood, wound with chains and charms, the
crown cut into the mark of their god. **About one cult witch in four carries one**
(the rest have a crude stick). It channels Chaos through the psyker who holds it.

**In a witch's hands:** each beat they use **one power**, and roll the
Perils trigger: **1-3**, then roll the Perils result (Codex, Gear rules).

| Power | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Warp-fire** | A gout of fire at one target: hits like a flamer and sets them alight (put it out next beat or drop a health step) |
| **Lightning** | Arcs to a target and one person beside them: ignores flak, and anyone hit takes -2 next beat |
| **Force blast** | Hits like a hellgun and throws the target off their feet: they're **knocked down** (in-hand weapons drop; drop-prone guns roll) |
| **Warp ward** | Blocks the first shot and the first melee hit at the witch this beat (a raw 17+ still gets through) |
| **Mind-lash** | One target in sight: WIS save or frozen for a beat; a purity seal gives advantage |

Wounding, stunning or disarming the witch breaks a power mid-beat.

### God powers: the charge and the summoning

A witch with a staff can call on their god for something bigger, but it takes
**a full beat to charge**. As the charge begins, the staff tears the air open and
**daemons spill out to guard the witch** while it builds. Next beat the god power
fires, unless the Light breaks the charge first (wound, stun or disarm the witch).
Breaking the charge stops the power, **but the daemons stay** until the witch goes
Down; then they fade at the end of that beat. The god power still rolls the Perils trigger once, when it fires.

| God | Summoned while charging | The god power (next beat) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nurgle** | **Nurglings:** a swarm of 4-6 giggling little rot-daemons. Each hit kills only one; their bites force a CON save or sickness | **Stream of corruption:** a cone ~8 m long; everyone in it makes a CON save or drops a health step and is sickened (antitoxin cures) |
| **Tzeentch** | **Blue horrors:** 2 sulking blue daemons that throw warp-fire bolts (hit like a lasgun). A slain blue horror **splits into two small brimstone horrors** that burn on touch | **Warp lightning storm:** arcs through up to 3 targets in a chain, ignoring carapace on a solid hit; each hit takes -2 next beat |
| **Slaanesh** | **Daemonettes:** 1-2 fast, lithe daemons with claws. +2 to strike first; anyone seeing them first makes a WIS save or takes -1 next beat (the allure) | **Lash of Slaanesh:** a whip of warp-force drags one target up to 6 m (into the open, off a ledge, to the daemonettes); WIS save or stunned a beat |
| **Undivided** | **Furies:** 2 winged, screaming daemons that dive from above (cover from the front doesn't help) | **Warp blast:** hits like a meltagun on one target, or a wave that knocks everyone within ~4 m down |

**Khorne has no witches:** the Blood God despises sorcery. Khorne cultists bring
daemons (Bloodletters, with hellblades that cut through carapace on any hit) only
through blood: a sacrifice or a grimoire chant, never a staff.

**Daemons and the Light:** daemons are warp-things. Force weapons and blessed
blades burn them; a purity seal gives advantage on any save their powers force;
they fade when their witch falls. Their loot is nothing: ash and a smell. **As loot:** useless to anyone but a psyker. Anyone else who carries it
makes a WIS save each day or hears it; the Inquisition wants it destroyed.

## Chaos ward-mark

(rare, 15 TV on the black market; player design.) A cult charm on a cord: a bone
disc, a scrap of skin or a brass token carrying the **mark of their god**. **The first
time a hit would drop the wearer a health step, the mark takes it, the god's way,
and fades** (the brand, ink or brass goes blank). Then it's spent.

| God | How it takes the hit | The price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Khorne** | No sorcery: a **brass blood-mark** drinks the blow (the Blood God's gift, not a spell). It also shrugs off one warp power | The wearer frenzies: next beat they must charge the nearest enemy |
| **Nurgle** | **Rot-vines** burst from the charm and catch the blow, then wither | The wearer stinks and weeps; -1 to sneak for the rest of the fight |
| **Slaanesh** | A pale **tentacle** lashes out of the seal and takes the hit | The wearer is dazed with pleasure: -1 on their next roll |
| **Tzeentch** | Fate twists: **the shot or blow simply misses**, however good it was | The DM may change one small thing in the scene, Tzeentch's way |
| **Undivided** | **The warp opens** for a heartbeat and swallows the shot or blow | Frost and whispers; everyone within ~3 m makes a WIS save or takes -1 next beat |

Cult leaders and witches carry them (found-or-made, rare). **As loot:** it can work
for one of the Light too, but wearing a god's mark is heresy: a purity seal
burns away near it, and the DM may make the wearer pay something later.

## Blessed cultists

(enemy rule, set by the player.) Any cultist, grunt or specialist, can be **blessed
by their god**. **Blessed is one of the cultist specialist types**, next to sniper,
heavy and the rest. A cultist who rolls specialist and draws *blessed* rolls the role
d20 **again**: grunt or specialist on the normal ranges. If specialist, draw a type
again; drawing *blessed* a second time makes them a blessed grunt. A blessed
cultist is easy to spot, and the Light should be able to tell what it means:

| God | How to tell | The blessing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Khorne** | Soaked in a dark, glossy glaze of blood that never dries | **+1 in melee**, and when they go Down they keep fighting for **one more beat** before they fall |
| **Nurgle** | Green, stinking vines growing through the skin; open plague sores | **The first hit on them does nothing** (the rot soaks it); their melee hits force a CON save or sickness |
| **Slaanesh** | Purple mist and a sickly sweet smell; small parts turned to tentacles (fingers, toes, an ear) | **-1 to hit them** (they move wrong, too fast), and +2 to strike first |
| **Tzeentch** | Feathers, a beak or claws growing out of them; the number 9 carved all over the skin | **Once per fight, a hit on them simply misses** (fate turns it), and they see the Light coming: no surprise on them |
| **Undivided** | The eight-pointed star branded across them, glowing faintly | **+1 on all their rolls** for the fight |

**Blessings stack with everything** (role rules, drugs, gear): a blessed
specialist keeps their full role and adds the blessing on top.

A purity seal gives advantage on any save a blessing forces. Blessed cultists are
the ones to shoot first, and their bodies are worth showing to the Inquisition.
