# Three Crowns — the story so far

Written to be read cold. If this campaign is picked up months from now, read this
file, then `README.md` in this folder for the hard state, then
`../../DND_CODEX.md` for the rules.

**Where we stopped:** day eight, morning, in a captured Dominion fort in the
valley, with eleven prisoners roped in the yard and a decision not yet made.

---

## The world in one paragraph

A medieval world with three powers and no winner. **The Concord** are knights
under one crown and one faith, holding the most land and the fewest friends.
**The Jarldoms** are the clans of the northern reach, no capital to besiege,
wealth off the water — Hallvard's people. **The Dominion** came across the
eastern sea two generations ago, rigid, patient, better steel. None of the three
can beat the other two at once.

Magic exists and always has. It is rare enough that a man can raid for twenty
years and never see it.

---

## Who Hallvard is

**Hallvard Twin-Axe.** Human, Jarldoms raider, level 4 Barbarian on the Path of
the Berserker. Fights with paired handaxes. From **Kolstad on the Vatn fjord**,
where his brothers and sisters still live, and where **Jarl Ingvar** — the man
who taught him the twin-axe style and is still better at it — holds the hall.

---

## What happened

**The wreck.** The longship *Sea-Wolf* went onto rocks in a storm on the Concord
coast. Hallvard woke face-down on the shingle. Sigvard, a crewman he'd known
twenty years, was dead on the sand — killed by a narrow clean thrust through the
back, not by the sea. A boy was dead beside him with a snapped Dominion bead in
his fist.

Hidden under Sigvard's tunic was a **sealed bronze dispatch tube**, Concord work,
wax intact.

**The waystation.** Hallvard went inland toward a column of smoke and found a
burned Concord waystation held by four Dominion soldiers, with his crewmate
**Ketil** bound and kneeling in the yard and a row of bronze tubes laid out on a
cloak. He blew the approach, was spotted, refused to surrender, ambushed the men
sweeping the brush and killed all three. The commanding officer never fought — he
bundled the tubes, mounted, and rode east.

In the waystation cellar, two days without water under a burning building, was
**Emeric**, a seventeen-year-old Concord courier, beside the body of his superior
**Brannoc**. Hallvard went down a rope for him.

**The twelve letters.** Emeric gave up what he knew. The Concord sent twelve
sealed letters to twelve northern jarls. Eleven were offers of alliance against
the Dominion. The twelfth — the one in Hallvard's shirt — was written separately
and sealed with a personal device rather than the Crown's.

Hallvard broke the seal and, being unable to read, handed it to the Concord boy.

The twelfth letter tells Ingvar the other eleven offers are a feint. That the
Concord will stand back in the spring while the Dominion comes up the fjords. And
that if Kolstad keeps its ships home and its mouth shut while the other eleven
halls burn, the Concord will recognise Ingvar as jarl over all of it.

The device on the wax is a **hawk on a broken wheel**: **Lord Aldous, the King's
brother**, who also wrote the eleven honest offers with the same hand.

Brannoc's dying words were *don't let them read the last one.* He had already
watched the Dominion take the other eleven when he said it.

**The oath.** At dusk, having learned that Ketil's home of **Hafnstad** is on the
burn list, Hallvard swore that no alliance would form, that every hall in the
north would hear the offer was a trap, and that if Ingvar reached for the crown
he was being handed, Hallvard would cut him down himself.

Ketil put his forehead against Hallvard's and said he wouldn't ask whether
Hafnstad was on the list, because he wanted to find out he'd stand there anyway.

**The fort.** Three miles east, up the valley, an old Concord fort the Dominion
had taken. Eight of the *Sea-Wolf*'s crew inside, roped at the neck in the
frost. Hallvard went in alone through an eighteen-inch water culvert with Emeric
behind him, cleared four men out of the stable, cut the rope line, and started a
fight in the open yard against fourteen.

Emeric ran sixty feet at an armed man to open the gate and was cut down five feet
short of it. Hallvard opened it himself and Ketil came through.

Hallvard sent his shield wall back to the gate, charged the officer alone, and
put him down. With his commander dying in the dirt and ten of his men dead, the
remaining eleven laid down their swords.

**The officer's last words**, given freely and unasked: the crewman everyone took
for a traitor, **Bersi**, had broken after capture and given them nothing. The
Dominion had known about the ship four days before it sailed, known the count of
the tubes, known the name of each hall — and it came from the Concord side, from
very high up.

**What Bersi saw.** Told to Thorbrand, with Hallvard present: on the steerboard
watch, something came up the weather side of the ship out of the water in the
dark. Man-shaped, and not a man. It put something through Sigvard where he slept,
took something heavy off the deck, and went back over the side with it. Bersi
told himself he'd dreamed it and said nothing for four hours, and then the storm
took them onto the rocks.

The deep drag furrow on the beach, out of the surf and inland, is where it went.
Hallvard stood over that furrow on the first morning and chose the smoke instead.

**Thorbrand's judgement on Bersi:** he lives, pulls an oar, eats last, and nobody
stands beside him in a wall again. Hallvard then privately told him he could earn
his way back, which quietly contradicts that sentence. Bersi volunteered to walk
point down the furrow.

**Emeric named all eleven halls** at the fire. Hafnstad is third. Halla's hall is
seventh, Steinar's ninth. **Kolstad is not on the list.** Every person at that
fire has a home due to burn except Hallvard, whose home is being offered a crown
to watch it happen. Nobody has said a word about that.

---

## The week after

- **Orm died.** The amputation turned septic on the second day and the fever took
  him on the fifth. Buried under stone on the slope above the fort.
- **Emeric lived**, barely, and will limp for life.
- **The eleven letters were never in the fort.** The officer sent them east by
  rider the same afternoon, hours before Hallvard arrived. They are days gone.
- **Day six: four Dominion riders** looked at the fort from a distance and rode
  east without approaching. The Dominion knows the place fell.

---

## The open decision

The fort cannot be held. Nine people, two of them limping, against whatever comes
back up that valley.

Three directions pull at once. **North** is Kolstad, Ingvar, and the letter.
**East** is the rider carrying the other eleven. **Back west** is the furrow on
the beach and whatever came out of the sea.

And eleven roped prisoners who cannot be fed, marched, killed, or safely
released.

That is where the table stopped.
