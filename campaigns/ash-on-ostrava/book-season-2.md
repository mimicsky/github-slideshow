# Part One: The Spindle

## Chapter 1: The Bright Light

The refit was finished on Saint Ivaine's Rest, and on the morning of departure
*The Bright Light* sat in her berth clamps grey with white stripes, her side
hangar open on a white Valkyrie with grey wings. Pilgrim bells rang up the spire.

Kesh came down the berth with Sister Aldwyn at her shoulder, and with her came
the people Tamsin had asked her for: two pilots and a tech-priest. Teo Brack was
young and wiry and chewing something, in a void-racer's patched jacket. Mira Dhal
was older, in a Navy flight suit with the insignia unpicked. Ferro-Ashe was red
robes and brass and odd wiring, with one green lens that whirred.

Between two Ecclesiarchy guards walked a woman in a grey prisoner's smock, with a
shaved head, smuggler's tattoos across her knuckles, and a penitent collar.

"Interrogator. One more for your collection," Kesh said. "Nessa Vane. Smuggler.
Black-marketeer. I was a smuggler's daughter. I know waste when I see it. You
seem to have a gift for making these people useful."

Nessa spat on the deck plates by his boots. "I'm not one of your soldiers,
Interrogator. Remember that when you sleep."

"Dace, take her down to cell one," Tamsin said, and when she had gone: "Yes,
ma'am. I'll see what I can do." Kesh dropped the collar's detonator into his
palm. "Don't let her near the vox."

He turned to the three at the foot of the ramp. "So you're my new pilots and my
new tech-priest. You are now part of the Light."

"Sure, I'll take it," Teo said. "Does that come with the paint?"

"I fly, Interrogator," Mira said. "I'm not a soldier."

"The Light. Photonic. Purposeful. Acceptable," said Ferro-Ashe. "The Mechanicus
did not want me. You do. That is data I will remember."

"Well, you're all part of the Light now," Tamsin said. "Welcome aboard. Come look
around." Light-pilots, both of them, and a Light-tech-priest, whatever they
thought of the title.

## Chapter 2: The List

On the briefing-room hololith Kesh had left five worlds. Tamsin looked at the
mining moon first, then chose the one closer to the bone: Ustrennos, Drachen's old
anchorage, where the Lord-General's last loyal men were said to be holding out
over his stores.

"We'll have to get authorization to clear them out," he told the Light, "and
once we do I suspect a last Gate Guard is in charge. It will be CQC, which is
what we're good at. But it may be more hostiles than we expect. So we'll have to
scout and make a move off that."

Corvane knew the place: Drachen's private yard was the Spindle, and the garrison
there had been Captain Severin Holt's. Mira did not like it. "Kesh told me
heretics. These are Guardsmen. Cadians. I didn't sign on to fly the Inquisition
into a Cadian civil war."

"These men have been told that he was a traitor," Tamsin said. "He opened the
gate on Cadia. They know, and there's proof. Are you saying if your lord was a
traitor you'd follow him?"

"No. I wouldn't," Mira said, and told him about the instructor she had put on the
deck. She asked that any who threw down their guns be given the chance. He
refused her, then gave it to her anyway: "Because you asked nicely, I'll try for
a non-lethal approach. This time."

He sent the whole wish list of the Light to Kesh as a requisition. She was in a
mood. *You are an Ordo officer, not a quartermaster's wish list.* She sent the
blessed antitoxin, the plague-ward filters, plasteel and fuel, and half of the
rest, and nothing that went bang. The mission plan she approved. *The
authorization is yours to win. Ustrennos is held by Commodore Hale Varn. He
declared for me third, not first. Remember that.*

## Chapter 3: Cargo

The *Bright Light* went into the warp with a cough of her old Geller generator,
and for four seconds the lower deck went dark. When Tamsin came down the stair
with Kell and Dace, cell one's maglock had let go and Nessa Vane was sitting on
the bottom step.

"Relax, Interrogator. Where would I run? We're in the warp."

Dace forged a tumbler lock for the door. Tamsin asked her if she was going to be
trouble, and she told him: treat her like cargo and she'd act like cargo. He told
her to prove herself, as Dace and Corvane had, and she said she'd start with the
smith.

He found Corvane in the shrine nook oiling the stub revolver, and Corvane told
him honestly how it felt to be one of them: like the only difference between him
and Holt's men was that Tamsin had caught him first. "When you came after us,"
Tamsin told him, "all you had was Drachen's word. They've seen the proof, and
they're still holding the Spindle for him. The difference is you chose
different." "I chose different," Corvane said. "Yes. I did."

In the container he gave Corvane the Gate Guard officer's power sword and a set of
green carapace to paint grey, and set aside a Gate Guard bayonet for the day Nessa
was ready. The Void Exchange crate turned out to be perfect: a Lucius-pattern
chain axe for Kell, kraks for Tavi with Mag Vashti's apology, a photon flash for
Hollis, and the reflex sight, which he gave to Varga for Mercy. "We can still run
the rematch," he said.

On the fifth night Dace called him down. Nessa had pried open the cell intercom
with a bunk spring. "I wanted to *listen*. Information's the only currency I've
got left." He cut the wires and told her the truth of where they were going, and
she gave him something back: men holed up for weeks still eat, so someone at the
anchorage was feeding them. Follow the cargo.

"Now tell me," he said, "what are you hiding from me, Nessa? You know I'm an
Interrogator, right? I had a hell of a teacher."

She told him. Two years of runs for a broker on Torvane's Mart called Orlen
Thask, who paid triple; and the open gate on the crate seals; and the last run,
forty crates of lasguns and mining charges to Sennet's Reach. "Regret is about
what happened to you. Shame is about who you were when you chose."

"Indeed," said Tamsin. "It seems now you carry it along with us."

## Chapter 4: The Commodore

The ship came out of the warp hard and wide of Ustrennos. The Spindle hung far
out past the remnant fleet, a dark ring with no lights. Commodore Varn invited the
Interrogator aboard the *Iron Litany*, and Tamsin went with Corvane, Kell, Varga,
and Nessa in her chains as a prisoner he might be handing over. The weapons he
left aboard his own ship; the knife in his boot he kept.

Varn was tired and old and honest about the Spindle: thirty or forty Gate Guard,
locked down since the broadcast. He had starved them out, he said, so no Cadian
would kill another in front of the fleet.

"If they are cut off, sir, then why are they still eating?"

Varn broke. His dockmaster, Tull Orsk, signed a salvage tender out every few
days, the *Grey Widow*, and Varn had looked at the logs and put them back in the
pile. He gave the Ordo his authorization in writing, and, when Tamsin asked for
it, everything in the Spindle by right of the Ordo. He would write his own report
to Kesh, all of it. Tamsin told him Kesh had forgiven worse, and Varn gave him his
hand. "If the Light ever needs a berth, a ship, or a friend at Ustrennos, you ask
for me."

## Chapter 5: Ring Three

The plans were late, and the night clerk in the archive watched Varn's lieutenant
pull them. Tamsin did not wait. He took the Valk to Ustrennos Station with Kell,
Corvane, Brenner, Isk and Nessa, and gave Nessa her knife and a bead.

Nessa's drop from a crawlway grille rang her chains off the steel, and the
dockmaster's clerk went for the alarm. Tamsin slid under the half-down shutter and
put a bolt from Last Word through the clerk's shoulder an inch from the stud.
"Sorry to shoot you, son," he told him, "but I need this to go quiet."

He went into Orsk's quarters alone with his boot knife and took the drunk
dockmaster off his bunk. On Orsk's desk were the Spindle's handshake, written out
so he wouldn't forget it, and a ledger of six weeks of Drachen's gold. Orsk told
them everything: ration crates, scrubbers, power cells, and lately black candles,
chalk, and a sealed crate from a broker named Thask. In the front office, Nessa
went silent.

"You don't want to die, right?" Tamsin said. "Then you're giving us what we need,
and you're going to help us out. And maybe, just maybe, I'll see what I can do."

## Chapter 6: The Grey Widow

At 0900 Orsk's five dockhands walked into Bay 3-14 and into a circle of
grey-and-white guns. "ON THE GROUND, INQUISITION ORDER." They went down without a
shot. The *Widow* lifted on time with six Light-bringers in her hold among the
ration crates and Orsk's own voice on the vox.

"*Widow*, outer ring. Salvage... for the gate."

"You sound like death, Orsk."

"Bad amasec. Old stock."

"The gate stands."

"...It stands."

Bay Two opened and closed behind them, and Sergeant Brann and seven Gate Guard
walked out bored to the ramp. "Drop it," Tamsin said. Hollis's photon flash took
the rear six. Tamsin shot the fast one through the shoulder, Brann's las-bolt
spattered off Tamsin's chest plate, Varga put one down by the thigh, and when
Morcant and Brann locked up chest to chest, Tamsin hit Brann from the side at a
dead run and flattened him. "That's it, Sergeant. Your fight is over."

Brenner opened the doors and played Kesh's broadcast across the ring, and it
barely landed. Holt answered it calmly on the ring vox. A rim gun put a round
through the Valk's port engine, and twelve Gate Guard came down the spoke with a
heavy stubber and open gates chalked on their plate, chanting.

## Chapter 7: The Lock

Tamsin sent Brenner alone down the rim crawl-tube to kill the gun and put the
rest behind three crates. "WE ARE THE LIGHT, AND WE HOLD THE LINE, SHINING THE
WAY OF FAITH. WE WILL NOT FALTER. WHAT ARE WE?" And they answered him, and so did
Kell's team in the circling Valk.

His impact frag took the doorway and the stubber cart. He put four shots into
four chargers and three went down. Morcant took a beam through the shoulder. The
fourth came over the crates with a chainsword and Tamsin parried it with
Shameholder and sent the Light through the hatch to Brenner. "I'll hold here. Go,
go, GO." Varga went last. "Don't you dare die, boss."

He cut the chainsword man down through the waist seam of his plate and took a
hellgun beam in the side for it. Then he stood on the crates, hurt, with Last Word
in his left hand. "IS THIS ALL YOU HAVE?" Two shots, two men down in the doorway.
The gun on the rim went quiet. He sat down on a crate and waited for his people
to come back.

## Chapter 8: The Gardener

At the top of the spoke a pressure bulkhead was dogged shut under a pict-eye.
Holt spoke through the grille: the men at the lock had not been his. Sergeant
Malk Dresh had been preaching to the men for weeks, and fifteen of them would
follow him into the warp. Holt would not fire on the Light while it dealt with
him.

"I don't give a shit. Tavi, blow it."

The first krak jammed the door. The second opened it, and a beam went through
Tavi as he turned away. In Bay Two, Varga had found the false-bottomed crate:
six vials of cloudy green, and a letter for the Gate Captain. *One for the lungs
of the Spindle. Five ride home on the tender.* "That will be a surprise to Kesh,"
Tamsin said, and had them locked in his safe.

Sallow could not stop Tavi's bleeding in the spoke. Tamsin sent him back and
walked past his own team, and then he ran, Shameholder in one hand and Last Word
in the other, down to the chanting. Fifteen marked men knelt before Drachen's
open vault. Dresh stood over a cracked iron casket with a green bulb pulsing in
it. "The runner. He comes to the garden alone. Grandfather is kind."

The impact frag took eight of them. The Light came off the ramp behind him and
took more. Tamsin thumbed Ottrick's overdrive and went into the last four, and the
howling teeth went through bayonets and plate. Dresh cut his palm on the bulb.
"*Bloom.*" Tamsin slapped on his rebreather and brought Shameholder down through
Dresh and the seed together.

The mist reached Dace and Isk through slipped seals. The antitoxin Kesh had sent
was in Sallow's bag.

## Chapter 9: Mercy

"Where are you, Holt? You come to me, or if I find you, it'll be your head."
Holt came down with his hands open and his last two men. Forty-six had held the
ring with him six weeks ago. "Do what you came to do." Tamsin walked past him.

Tavi lived, by a finger's width. On the *Bright Light*, Tamsin put his fist into
the medicae bulkhead. "I said it would be easy. I said no one would get hurt.
And I got my team hurt." Sallow told him what failing looked like and that today
she had carried no one out in a bag. "That isn't failing. That's the job. It just
hurts." From the cot Tavi mumbled, "Told you it'd open, boss."

The Light stripped the Spindle to its bones. On Ustrennos Station thirty
dockhands came at the *Widow's* ramp to free their dockmaster. Tamsin stood
over them with Shameholder snarling and told them about the vials, and Orsk told
them it was true, and the wrench hit the deck. Tamsin gave most of the prisoners
to the station watch and asked that Orsk's help weigh on his sentence. "You said
*maybe*," Orsk said. "Thank you, Interrogator."

On the flight back the one cultist they had taken alive began to cough green.
Tamsin held out Shameholder's purity seal and chanted what he remembered of
Thane's words, and the seal burned away and the sickness pulled back, for a
while. The antitoxin would not take. Trooper Edrik Halm of Kasr Tyrok told them
what Dresh had promised, and where Thask could be found, and one more line from
the letter: *The first garden grows under the ice on Velk, where the choir
sleeps. It waits for a gardener to wake it.*

"You can't stop it. Can you."

"May the Emperor forgive you," Tamsin said, and Last Word was gentle.

He put Holt and Brann in cell two, gave Nessa the top bunk by the door, and took
the bayonet back. "Good work out there, Nessa. Thanks, assassin." In his cabin he
wrote two reports for Kesh, one about the mission and one about the Gardener,
and told her he was coming home with two prisoners and six vials.

## Chapter 10: Waiting on Kesh

That night Tamsin carried the Kasr Holn cogitator core from the galley stores aft
to Ferro-Ashe and laid Vigil on the bench. "Korren's breath," he said, and then
he bargained for more: a wider edge, paid for in heat. Ferro-Ashe chanted over
the gun all night and woke him at five.

"Korren would be proud of you," Tamsin told the rifle, hand on the stock. "I
appreciate you teaching me his ways, how to shoot better. I won't let you
overheat. You know that."

"Agitation: zero," said Ferro-Ashe. "It wanted to hear the last bearer *named*.
Remembered. By you. I hear numbers where Hespa-Vor heard voices. But that one I
almost heard." Shameholder, read the same way, had hated the seed more than
anything in its records, and did not want its old name back.

In the briefing room he told the Light he was disappointed, in himself and in
them: missed shots, short grenades, slow cover, a door that should have been
read before it was blown. Hollis owned his frags. Isk owned his misses. From his
cot Tavi owned the door. "I'll learn every door there is." Kell took the drills.
Tamsin gave each of them their trade to train, and Dace the job of peeling for
the others.

He gave Isk the Gate Guard marksman's long hellgun, and Morcant the combat pump.
Through the fleet stores he bought, with Drachen's gold, an auspex and a
cogitator spike for Brenner, a shock maul for Morcant, and a breaching charge
that Morcant laid on Tavi's chest like flowers. "Not on the bed, Tavi!"

With Brenner, Nessa and Ferro-Ashe he walked the empty ring one last time and
pulled a Mechanicus coupling out of the Spindle's power plant. The plant groaned
and died, and every light on the ring went out behind them. Ferro-Ashe started
on the Geller generator: two days. Tamsin told Kell the next blessing was his.
"Thanks, brother."

He shot Vigil on the range until Isk called it unfair, and then let Dace throw
ration tins at him from ten metres while he learned to be down again before the
count of two. Varga tried it and caught one on the ribs. "Not a word, boss."

"I'll let you get a little better," Tamsin said, "so when we have our duel it's
more fair for you, too."

**Where we stopped:** ~1445, warp day 8, the *Bright Light* docked at the dead
Spindle, waiting on Kesh's answer.
