# D&D Codex

The standing rulebook for any game I run for you. I am the DM, you are the
player. This is not tied to one campaign: it carries across every game we play.

Based on **D&D 5th Edition (2014 rules)**. Parts 1 to 5 are the rules. Part 6 is
the part you control. Part 7 is how I run a table. Part 8 is how the same rules
run a Warhammer 40,000 game.

Campaign-specific things — your character sheet, NPCs, session logs — live in a
separate file per campaign, not in here.

## Part 1 — The Core Rules

### The one mechanic that runs everything

Almost every uncertain action resolves the same way:

```
d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus (if proficient)  vs.  a target number
```

The target number is either a **DC** (Difficulty Class, set by the DM) or an
**AC** (Armor Class, when you attack something). Meet it or beat it and you
succeed. Ties go to the roller: rolling exactly the DC is a success.

> **House rule 1 applies here.** On ability checks, missing by 1 or 2 is a
> partial success rather than a flat failure. See Part 6.

There are three flavors of this roll, and they are mechanically identical:

| Roll | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| Ability check | Doing something hard: climbing, lying, picking a lock |
| Attack roll | Hitting a target with a weapon or spell |
| Saving throw | Resisting something happening *to* you |

### Ability scores and modifiers

Six scores. The score itself barely matters; the **modifier** is what you add to
rolls.

| Ability | Short | Governs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strength | STR | Raw power, melee attacks, carrying, athletics |
| Dexterity | DEX | Agility, stealth, ranged attacks, AC, initiative |
| Constitution | CON | Health, stamina, concentration saves |
| Intelligence | INT | Recall, deduction, arcane knowledge |
| Wisdom | WIS | Perception, insight, willpower |
| Charisma | CHA | Force of personality, social pressure, some magic |

Modifier = (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down.

| Score | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modifier | −1 | +0 | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 |

### Proficiency bonus

Added only to things your character is trained in. It scales with your **total
character level**, not your class.

| Level | 1–4 | 5–8 | 9–12 | 13–16 | 17–20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bonus | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 | +6 |

### The 18 skills

Each skill is tied to one ability. If you're proficient in a skill, add your
proficiency bonus to checks with it.

| Ability | Skills |
| --- | --- |
| STR | Athletics |
| DEX | Acrobatics, Sleight of Hand, Stealth |
| INT | Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion |
| WIS | Animal Handling, Insight, Medicine, Perception, Survival |
| CHA | Deception, Intimidation, Performance, Persuasion |

### Typical difficulty classes

| Task | DC |
| --- | --- |
| Very easy | 5 |
| Easy | 10 |
| Medium | 15 |
| Hard | 20 |
| Very hard | 25 |
| Nearly impossible | 30 |

### Advantage and disadvantage

Roll **two d20s**. Advantage: keep the higher. Disadvantage: keep the lower.

Three things worth memorizing:

1. They do not stack. Three sources of advantage is still just advantage.
2. Any advantage and any disadvantage cancel out completely, and you roll one
   plain d20.
3. They apply to the d20 only, never to damage dice.

### Saving throws

Same formula, but you're resisting. Every class is proficient in exactly two
saving throws. Common ones:

- **DEX** — dodging fireballs, traps, area damage
- **CON** — poison, disease, holding concentration on a spell
- **WIS** — charms, fear, mind control

"Half damage on a successful save" is the most common outcome for area spells.

## Part 2 — Combat

*Reference only. In narrative mode (house rule 4) initiative, turns, AC and HP are switched off; fights use Part 6 (the exchange, shot chains, Cover, Shots and hits, Breaking and Down).*

### Turn order

Everyone rolls **initiative** once at the start: d20 + DEX modifier. Highest
goes first. That order repeats every round. One round is six seconds of game
time.

**At our table:** the player rolls individually. Monsters roll in groups, so all
six goblins share one initiative count and act together. Ties break in the
player's favor.

**Surprise:** a surprised creature can't move or act on its first turn and can't
take reactions until that turn ends. It is one lost turn, not a free round.

### What you get on your turn

| Resource | How many | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | Up to your speed (usually 30 ft.) | Can be split before and after your action |
| Action | 1 | The main thing you do |
| Bonus action | 1 | Only if a feature or spell specifically grants one |
| Free interaction | 1 | Draw a sword, open a door, pull a lever |
| Reaction | 1 per round | Usable on anyone's turn, refreshes at your turn start |

You do not get a bonus action by default. It has to come from somewhere.

### Common actions

| Action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Attack | One melee or ranged attack (more if your class grants Extra Attack) |
| Cast a Spell | Any spell with a casting time of 1 action |
| Dash | Double your movement this turn |
| Disengage | Your movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks |
| Dodge | Attacks against you have disadvantage; your DEX saves have advantage |
| Help | Give an ally advantage on one check or attack |
| Hide | Make a Stealth check to become unseen |
| Ready | Prepare a trigger: "when X happens, I do Y" — uses your reaction |
| Search | Actively look for something |
| Use an Object | Interact with a second object, or use a magic item |
| Grapple / Shove | Replace one attack: your Athletics vs. their Athletics or Acrobatics |

### Opportunity attacks

When a hostile creature you can see **leaves your reach** using its movement,
you may use your reaction to make one melee attack against it.

It does not trigger if the creature Disengages, teleports, or is moved against
its will (pushed, yanked, shoved).

### Attack rolls, hits, and crits

```
d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus  vs.  target's AC
```

Use STR for melee weapons, DEX for ranged and finesse weapons, and your
spellcasting ability for spell attacks.

- **Natural 20** — automatic hit and a critical. Roll all the *damage dice*
  twice, then add your modifier once. The modifier is never doubled.
- **Natural 1** — automatic miss, no matter your bonuses.

### Damage, healing, and dying

At **0 hit points** you fall unconscious and start making **death saving
throws** on each of your turns: a flat d20, no modifiers.

| Result | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 10 or higher | One success |
| 9 or lower | One failure |
| Natural 20 | You regain 1 HP and get back up immediately |
| Natural 1 | Two failures |

Three successes and you're stable. Three failures and you die. Successes and
failures both reset when you're healed or stabilized.

Other things to remember:

- Any healing above 0 HP wakes you up and clears the tally.
- Taking damage at 0 HP is one automatic failure; a crit is two.
- A DC 10 Medicine check stabilizes a dying ally without healing them.
- **Massive damage**: if a single hit reduces you to 0 and the leftover damage
  equals or exceeds your HP maximum, you die outright.
- Hit points never go above your maximum, and never below 0.

### Cover

*Superseded by Part 6, Cover (+1 / +2 / +4 to the hidden target).*

| Cover | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Half (low wall, another creature) | +2 AC and +2 DEX saves |
| Three-quarters (arrow slit, portcullis) | +5 AC and +5 DEX saves |
| Total | Cannot be targeted directly at all |

### Movement details

- **Difficult terrain** costs 2 feet of movement per foot traveled.
- **Standing up from prone** costs half your total speed.
- **Climbing or swimming** costs an extra foot per foot, unless you have a
  climb or swim speed.
- You can move through an **ally's** space freely, and through a hostile
  creature's space only if it's two sizes larger or smaller than you.

## Part 3 — Conditions

The ones that decide fights. "Incapacitated" is the key word: it means no
actions and no reactions at all.

| Condition | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Blinded | Auto-fail sight checks; your attacks have disadvantage, attacks on you have advantage |
| Charmed | Can't attack the charmer; the charmer has advantage on social checks with you |
| Deafened | Auto-fail hearing checks |
| Frightened | Disadvantage on checks and attacks while the source is in sight; can't move closer to it |
| Grappled | Speed becomes 0 |
| Incapacitated | No actions and no reactions |
| Invisible | Heavily obscured; your attacks have advantage, attacks on you have disadvantage |
| Paralyzed | Incapacitated, can't move or speak, auto-fail STR and DEX saves, attacks on you have advantage, and any hit from within 5 ft. is a critical |
| Petrified | Turned to stone, incapacitated, resistant to all damage, immune to poison and disease |
| Poisoned | Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks |
| Prone | Disadvantage on your attacks; melee attacks on you have advantage, ranged attacks on you have disadvantage |
| Restrained | Speed 0, your attacks have disadvantage, attacks on you have advantage, disadvantage on DEX saves |
| Stunned | Incapacitated, can't move, can barely speak, auto-fail STR and DEX saves, attacks on you have advantage |
| Unconscious | Incapacitated and prone, drops everything, auto-fail STR and DEX saves, attacks on you have advantage, hits from within 5 ft. are critical |

### Exhaustion (2014 rules)

Stacks in six levels, and each level includes the ones below it.

| Level | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Disadvantage on ability checks |
| 2 | Speed halved |
| 3 | Disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws |
| 4 | Hit point maximum halved |
| 5 | Speed reduced to 0 |
| 6 | Death |

A long rest with food and water removes one level.

> **2024 change:** exhaustion was simplified to −2 on every d20 roll per level
> and −5 feet of speed per level, with death still at level 6. Confirm with your
> DM which version you're using.

## Part 4 — Spellcasting

### The two numbers every caster needs

```
Spell save DC   = 8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier
Spell attack    = d20 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier
```

Your spellcasting ability depends on class: INT for wizards and artificers, WIS
for clerics, druids, and rangers, CHA for bards, sorcerers, warlocks, and
paladins.

### Slots

- Casting a spell of level 1 or higher spends a slot of that level or higher.
- Casting with a **higher-level slot** often improves the spell. Check the
  "At Higher Levels" line.
- **Cantrips** are level 0, cost no slot, and are unlimited. They scale
  automatically at character levels 5, 11, and 17.
- Most slots come back on a long rest. Warlocks are the exception and recover
  theirs on a short rest.

### Concentration

The rule that trips up every new caster.

- You can concentrate on only **one** spell at a time. Starting a second one
  ends the first immediately.
- When you take damage while concentrating, make a **CON save**. The DC is 10,
  or half the damage taken, whichever is higher.
- Concentration also breaks if you're incapacitated or killed.

### Other spellcasting notes

- **Components** — V (verbal, you must speak), S (somatic, a free hand), M
  (material). A component pouch or focus covers any material with no listed
  cost. Materials with a gold cost must be bought for real.
- **Rituals** — spells tagged as rituals can be cast without a slot if you take
  10 extra minutes.
- **One-spell-per-turn limit** — if you cast a spell as a bonus action, the only
  other spell you can cast that turn is a cantrip with a casting time of 1
  action.

## Part 5 — Rests, Recovery, and Leveling

### Short rest — at least 1 hour

Spend any number of your **Hit Dice**. For each one, roll the die, add your CON
modifier, and regain that many hit points (minimum 0). You have one Hit Die per
character level, and the die size is set by your class.

### Long rest — at least 8 hours

- Regain **all** hit points.
- Regain spent Hit Dice equal to **half your total**, rounded down, minimum 1.
- Regain spell slots and most class features.
- Remove one level of exhaustion, if you ate and drank.
- You only benefit from one long rest per 24 hours, and you need at least 6
  hours of sleep with no more than 2 hours of light activity.

### Leveling up

Two systems. Ask your DM which one you're on.

- **XP** — you accumulate experience points from encounters and hit thresholds.
- **Milestone** — the DM levels the party when the story reaches a beat. Simpler
  and more common in AI-run and one-shot games.

On level up: increase your hit point maximum (roll your class Hit Die and add
your CON modifier, or take the fixed average), add a Hit Die, recalculate your
proficiency bonus if it changed, and take any new class features. At levels 4,
8, 12, 16, and 19 you get an **Ability Score Improvement**: +2 to one score, +1
to two scores, or a feat instead if your table uses them.

### How the player likes to play

Standing preferences, set during play. They apply to every campaign.

- **Let the player figure it out.** A clue is shown **once**, plainly, then left
  alone. Don't repeat it, don't have NPCs bring it up again or point at it, and
  don't nudge toward the answer. The player puts it together. If they're stuck,
  they'll say so out of character, and only then does the DM help. Set by the
  player, Ash on Ostrava session 9.
- **Stop after the player's action.** Resolve only what the player does, then
  hand control back. NPCs don't make big moves past it.
  **Don't move, read, think or feel for the player character either** (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10). Example: the player says *"I grab the slate and walk out of the room."* Wrong: the character reads the roster on the way, crosses names off in his head, and heads somewhere. Right: he picks up the slate, walks out, and the scene stops at the door, with what he'd see there; what he does with the slate is the player's next move.
- **Stop at the first new decision point.** When an action has several steps ("let it shut, then we breach"), resolve up to the first moment where things change and the player could choose differently (a door shuts, a breach is about to start), then stop. The player may also say where to stop. Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 6.
- **In fights, everyone acts every beat.** Allies and enemies each take their
  own action with their own open roll, every beat, not just the ones the player
  named. The pause-after-the-player rule is about story moves, not combat
  turns. Set by the player, session 5.
- **Matches and contests go round by round** (shootouts, bouts, races): roll one round, show it, stop for the player (who may coach or react), then the next. Never run a whole match in one go. **In mat bouts a "round" is a touch:** play the blocks and parries straight through and stop only when a touch lands. Mat bouts use blunt practice swords only (no shields, no specialties): d20 + STR each. **Knife bouts** (blunted knives, chalked edges; no specialties): **d20 + DEX each**; blocks are **slips**, parries are **evade-and-counter**; a landed strike to hand, arm or leg is **minor (1 hit)**, to head, neck, torso or back is **vital (2 hits)**: vital on a **raw 17+ or a win by 5+**, otherwise minor; **first to 2 hits wins the round, first to 5 rounds wins the bout** (in Ash on Ostrava, Dace and Hollis judge). In real fights knives use the better of STR or DEX (house rule 5, "Which stat"). Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10.
- **Speech in quotes** is the character talking aloud; everything else is action.
- **Narrate the player character in second person** ("you"), not his name in the third person, in scenes. (The book stays third person.) Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10.
- **No game terms in the fiction.** NPCs never mention rolls, dice, natural 20s, DCs or modifiers; they describe what they saw. Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10.
- **Name + role, every time.** Every time a named NPC, place or item comes up,
  give the name then what it is, e.g. Hespa-Vor (enginseer), Velk (ice world),
  Officer (Kell's hellgun). Every mention, not just the first.
- **Enemies carry their role tag** (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10). In a fight, every enemy with a bestiary role is named by a short descriptor plus the role, every mention, so the player knows who is who: *Coat guy (gunslinger)*, *Chain-axe guy (berserker)*, *Flamer man (flamer)*; rank and file are *(grunt)*. A named enemy gets both: *Old Wick (Tallow Boys boss, leader)*.
- **Dialogue as a script.** Speech is written in play format, one line per
  speaker: `Kell (sergeant): line`. Action and description stay as prose
  around it. Set by the player, session 4.
- **Describe places in detail, for free,** when asked. Perception is only for
  hidden things. **No text maps;** a drawn picture of the space (a rendered image) is fine when the player asks. Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 6.
- **The player rolls physical dice** and gives the raw number; the DM adds
  modifiers. NPC dice and undecided facts are rolled openly with a real RNG.
- **Shop stock only on a visit.** Don't list a quartermaster's or trader's
  stock in the morning summary; show it only when the character goes to them.
  Set by the player, session 5.
- **No repeats.** Save before writing the scene; the save note is just
  "(saved)" at the top, never a recap. The scene is the last thing in the reply.
  NPCs don't re-explain what they already said, and the time and situation
  aren't restated when nothing changed. Set by the player, session 5.
- **The world moves off-screen.** The first time the character meets someone
  after a gap (hours or days), say what that person has been doing meanwhile:
  **training** (logged as their daily rep), **something ordinary** (sleeping,
  cards, writing home), or **something that matters to the story** (talked to a
  prisoner, found something, had a falling-out). Weigh it by what's going on:
  if the player has ordered training, they mostly train; the wounded rest;
  people follow their own worries. Roll openly when it's genuinely undecided.
  Set by the player, session 5.
- **Don't roll ordinary dialogue.** NPCs answer from their own character, history
  and what they know. Rolls are for real turning points, real refusals and
  genuinely undecided facts, not for how a conversation goes. Set by the player,
  Ash on Ostrava session 8.
- **NPCs only know what they could know.** A character knows something only if
  they saw it, were told it, or heard it from someone who could have; if it's
  gossip, the fiction shows who passed it on. No one knows a thing just because
  the player does. Set by the player, session 5.
- **Listeners only understand what they could know, too.** When an NPC mentions a person, place or event, the listener reacts as someone who knows it or doesn't (a prisoner doesn't know who Corvane is; the NPC either explains or the listener is lost). Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10.
- **Clean up as we go (set by the player, session 9).** The campaign sheet holds
  current state only; during play, delete or shorten anything closed or no longer
  useful in the same save, instead of letting it pile up.
- **Daily tally sheet (set by the player, session 9).** Whenever the game clock
  passes into a new day, show a tally of the day just ended before the next scene:
  each person's reps (what they did, hours or actions, +0.1 each) and their new stat
  levels. The player wants to see progress, not take it on trust.
- **Show the math.** Every roll is written as (die + bonuses = total), e.g.
  (12+6+1+2 = 21): the die, the stat bonus, then each other bonus, then the total.
  Contests: (ours = total vs theirs = total). DCs stay hidden. Set by the player,
  Ash on Ostrava session 9 (replacing 'don't show the math', session 5).
- **Hidden DCs, near-miss partials, the exchange** for melee (house rules 1, 3, 5).
- **Explain setting lore briefly** out of character when asked, limited to what
  the character would know. Use references the player knows (Darktide for 40k).
- **The player is a collector.** Put interesting things in the world to find and
  keep; invented gear is fine if it fits the setting.
- **Fewer freebies.** Gear is earned through salvage, trades and risk.
- **The player may propose** blessings, mods and backstory; the DM judges fairness.
- **Save state** to the campaign files and push after anything important.
- **Full kit (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).** A campaign keeps a **Full kit** table (in its campaign sheet): each person's standing loadout. 'Full kit for everyone' means exactly that table, unless the player says otherwise for a mission. **Keep it current in the same save:** whenever something is used up, lost, swapped, fitted, found or handed out (ammo, grenades and attachments too), change its row; don't add gear the player hasn't assigned. **A campaign's shared container is open stock: anyone may take from it if they want; update both the person's row and the container's count.** Power ratings are re-run from it.

### House rules

Rules we changed on purpose. I add a line here every time we make a ruling that
differs from the book, so we stay consistent later.

**1. Near-miss partial success on ability checks.**

If an ability check misses the DC by **1 or 2**, it is not a flat failure. You
get what you wanted in a reduced or costly form, and I narrate the cost.

| Check | DC 15, you roll 13 or 14 |
| --- | --- |
| Athletics to climb | You reach the top, winded, and something falls from your pack |
| Perception to scan a room | You catch one thing, not everything |
| Stealth past a guard | You get through, but he half-noticed something |
| Persuasion on a merchant | He agrees, at a worse price or with a condition |

Scope of the rule:

- **Ability checks only.** Attack rolls and saving throws are untouched.
- **Attack rolls** keep meet-it-beats-it. A tie hits. Attacks already have
  granular outcomes through damage rolls and crits, and shaving 5% off every
  attack would quietly punish martial characters far more than casters.
- **Saving throws** are untouched. Half damage on a successful save is already
  a partial-success system.
- A natural 1 on an ability check is still just a low roll, not an auto-fail,
  so the near-miss band never triggers off it.

**2. Variant ability pairing on skills.**

A skill is normally locked to one ability. Under this rule, when the *way* a
character attempts something clearly runs on a different ability, I swap the
ability and keep the skill.

| Pairing | When it applies |
| --- | --- |
| STR + Intimidation | Menacing through raw physical threat rather than words |
| INT + Stealth | Planning a route around patrols rather than moving quietly |
| CON + Performance | An endurance act: an all-night song, a drinking contest |
| WIS + Deception | Reading what someone wants to hear and feeding it back |

How it works:

- Skill proficiency still applies normally. Only the ability modifier changes.
- **I call it, not the player.** The default pairing stands unless the action
  clearly isn't the standard one. This is not a menu to shop from for whichever
  ability happens to be highest.
- A player may suggest a pairing, and I either take it or name the one that fits.

**3. Hidden DCs.**

I don't announce the target number. The player rolls, states the total, and I
narrate what happens. This keeps the tension in the fiction rather than in the
arithmetic.

The trade-off is deliberate: the player can't calculate whether a resource is
worth spending, and decides on instinct and my description of the situation
instead. I compensate by describing obstacles in enough detail to judge them.

**4. Narrative mode.**

The default way we play. The rules in Parts 1 to 5 stay in this book as
reference, but they are not run at the table unless we switch modes.

What is switched off:

- Initiative, turn order, and rounds
- The action / bonus action / movement economy
- Armor Class and to-hit arithmetic
- Opportunity attacks, cover, and positioning in feet
- Hit points as a running number

What stays on:

- **One d20 at genuine turning points**, not once per exchange. A fight is two
  or three rolls, not six rounds.
- **Social scenes get rolled too.** Any time the other person has a real reason
  to say no, or the player is taking a genuine risk with someone, that is a roll.
  Not a check on whether an ally likes them, but a real test whenever refusal,
  anger or betrayal is actually on the table. Added at the player's request in
  session 1, after an oath that should have been rolled and wasn't.
- **The character sheet as fiction.** Hallvard being strong, tough and fast
  still decides what I call easy and what I call hard. The numbers inform me,
  they just don't get announced.
- **Real rolls, real consequences.** I still roll honestly and a bad roll still
  costs something. Death is still possible, it just arrives through the story
  rather than through a hit point total.
- **Hidden DCs, told saves, no option menus.** All unchanged.

**Health as a state, not a number** (penalties set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10):

| State | Meaning | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine | Untouched or scratched | none |
| Hurt | Bleeding, slowed, still dangerous | **-1 on rolls** |
| Badly hurt | One more bad exchange from going down | **-2 on rolls, no sprint** (a short move only) |
| Down | Out of the fight unless a medic patches them back to Hurt. **Enemies:** d6 after the fight, 1-3 dead. **The Light:** alive; dies only if finished off or hit again while Down (Part 6, Breaking and Down) | out |

**Some roles and gear add more, so check them** (each in its own entry):
- **Extra health stages** (each a free step before Hurt, no penalty): **Zealot** +1 (Grazed), **Skitarius** +1 (Grazed),
  **Ogryn** +3 (Grazed, Bruised), **Brute / mutant** +2. All in `wh40k_gear/bestiary/roles.md`.
- **Ignoring the penalties:** a **stimm** ignores Hurt while it lasts; a **concentrated stimm** makes Badly hurt fight as
  Hurt; **rot-stimm** ignores Hurt and Badly hurt; **berserker** and **chem-doctor** doses ignore Hurt (`drugs.md`;
  roles.md, Berserker and Chem-doctor).
- **Keeping on after Down:** **Khorne-blessed** fight one more beat; berserkers with doses left fight a beat after Down
  (`heretic.md`, Blessed cultists; roles.md, Berserker).
- **Soaking hits:** **Nurgle-blessed**, the first hit does nothing; **Tzeentch-blessed**, one hit a fight misses
  (`heretic.md`); **Tamsin's Defense style**, some hits that would make him Hurt only shake him (campaign sheet).
- **Extra steps on a hit:** some weapons drop the target one extra step (their catalogue entries); a **crit** always does
  (Part 6, Shots and hits).

The honest trade-off, stated so it isn't a surprise later: when dice stop
deciding every exchange, the DM decides more. Some tension becomes theatrical.
Rolling at the real turning points is what keeps it from becoming a story the
player is merely watching.

Switching back to full rules is a sentence at any time.

**5. The exchange: opposed rolls, parry and block.**

Replaces static defense in fights that matter. Melee becomes a back-and-forth
where defense is an action rather than a number.

**How one exchange works.** The attacker rolls. The defender rolls. Compare the
totals:

| Defender's total vs attacker's | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Lower or equal | The attack lands |
| 1 or 2 higher | **Block.** No damage. The attacker keeps initiative and swings again **next beat** |
| 3 or more higher | **Parry.** No damage, and **initiative flips.** The defender swings **next beat** |

**The unparryable blow.** A **raw d20 of 17 or higher** cannot be parried or
blocked at all. It lands. This is the raw die, not the total — otherwise the
threshold means something different for every character depending on their
modifier.

**Scope, and why it is limited:**

- **Any individual opponent.** Named or nameless, officer or sentry. There is no
  "important enough to duel" test — if you are trading blows with a person, you
  are in an exchange.
- **Mobs stay narrative.** When a crowd is pressing rather than individuals
  trading, the DM describes the pressure and does not roll an opposed die per
  body in it.
- **One swing a beat** (Part 6, A beat): each exchange above is one swing and its defence. **A defender can answer two attackers a beat.** The third and any beyond
  get through undefended.
- **Two weapons turn a block into a parry.** Fighting with a weapon in each hand
  does not raise the two-attacker cap — a second blade covers another line, it
  does not double how many people you can track. What it does is convert every
  block into a parry: the off hand catches the blow and the other weapon is
  already free, so initiative flips instead of merely holding. This is the
  mechanical identity of the twin-axe style, and it is lost the moment one axe
  is given away.
- **Position decides how many can reach you.** Space, not willingness, limits a
  crowd. In open ground three or four can get at one man, so he answers two and
  the rest land. In a gateway, doorway or corridor only one or two can reach him
  at all, and he can answer every one of them.

That last point is the tactical weight of the rule. Holding a door stops being
flavour and becomes the correct answer to being outnumbered, and walking out
into the open stops being brave and becomes arithmetic.

**Which stat the exchange uses (finesse; set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).**
Both sides roll the stat their weapon runs on, attacking *and* defending:

| Weapon | Stat |
| --- | --- |
| **Finesse:** knives, daggers, bayonets used in hand, short blades, light sabres | **the better of STR or DEX** |
| **Heavy:** chainswords, axes, mauls, hammers, power fists, long blades (e.g. Promise Holder) | **STR** |
| Unarmed: grappling and shoving | STR |
| Unarmed: slipping and dodging | DEX |

**Knife defence is footwork, not blade-on-blade:** with a knife, a *block* (1-2 higher)
is narrated as a **slip** (dodged, the attacker keeps going) and a *parry* (3+ higher) as
an **evade and counter** (dodged or the arm checked, initiative flips). Same math.
**Knife bouts on the mat** (blunted bayonets, chalked edges) use **d20 + DEX each** and
their own scoring: see "Matches and contests" in Part 6, How the player likes to play.

**6. Shot chains.**

Added at the player's request in session 5, Ash on Ostrava, because the dice had
felt too kind. When anyone fires more than one shot in a string at a moment that
matters, every shot is its own d20, and each result tilts the next.

| Shot | Hit | Miss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First shot | +2 to the next shot | -1 to the next shot |
| Every later shot | +1 to the next shot | -1 to the next shot |

- **No stacking.** Only the previous shot's result applies, so the modifier is
  always between -1 and +2.
- A plain bonus or penalty, **not** 5e advantage.
- **Enemies use it too.**
- Normal crits still apply (Tamsin: 19-20). The near-miss rule does not apply,
  because these are attack rolls.
- **Narrative mode still governs.** Mobs stay narrative; the chain is for
  strings of aimed shots at turning points, not every trigger pull.

**7. Stat levels and training reps.**

Added at the player's request in session 5, Ash on Ostrava. Replaces 5e ability
scores and Part 8's once-per-stay training.

- Each of the six stats runs **1 to 100**. Every full 10 levels is **+1** to
  rolls using that stat (level 40 Strength = +4).
- Converting old scores keeps the old modifier: level = (score - 10) x 5,
  minimum 1.
- **Proficiency stacks on top** as before and still rises with character level.
- **Training reps (revised by the player, end of session 9).** **+0.1 per hour of
  training** in a stat, and **+0.1 per action in play** that counts (a kill, a
  successful roll, a hit taken and stayed up through, an order, a clever play). No
  daily ladder, no cap. **Everyone who trains earns it: the player and every ally.**
  The DM tallies at day's end. A normal ship day on the training plan counts as
  **6 hours** of the plan's drill (+0.6) unless the scene says otherwise. See the
  training manual below for what counts.
- Sharp edges already earned under the old rule stay until used.

**Training manual (set by the player, session 9).** What counts. **Training: +0.1 per
hour. In play: +0.1 per action.** The DM tallies the player's and the Light's reps at
day's end, from the training plan and from what happened in play.

| Stat | Counts as training |
| --- | --- |
| STR | Working out (lifting, bodywork, anything mostly muscle), sparring and mat matches, melee drills (chain axe on the post, sword forms), hauling heavy kit or crates, carrying a wounded man, climbing, breaching by hand; **a successful STR roll** in play (e.g. breaking the frozen clamp); **a melee kill** |
| DEX | Range work, quick-draw drills, throwing knives or grenades at marks, stealth and crawlway practice, flying (pilots), lift-pack hops, fine handwork (lockpicking, trigger work); **a successful DEX roll** in play (a dodge, a dive, a clean draw); **a shot or thrown kill** (gun, thrown knife, grenade) |
| CON | Runs and heavy-kit marches, cold, void and gas drills, holding a post through a long watch in bad conditions; **a successful CON roll** in play (beating poison, plague, gas, cold); **taking a hit and staying up**: a hit that only shakes you (it bounces off) or drops you just one health step (not two, not Down) |
| INT | Reading manuals, cipher and vox work, repairs and crafting, studying plans and maps, reading slates and ledgers for intel; **a successful INT roll** in play; **DM's call: a clever play** (the player thinks of something genuinely smart that works: a trick, a plan, a deduction, a use of the ground; no roll needed). The DM marks it in the scene as *(clever play: INT rep)* |
| WIS | Coaching others, **commanding** (+0.1 per order given in play; anyone who gives orders: the player, Kell, Varga), treating the sick and wounded, tracking, standing a real watch, reading people in an interrogation, prayer and vigil; **a successful WIS roll** in play (Perception, Insight, resisting fear or the warp) |
| CHA | Briefings and speeches, bargaining and trading, interrogation talk, leading a drill, recruiting, **a deep talk** (a real heart-to-heart, not small talk); **a successful CHA roll** in play (a bluff, a threat, a persuade); **DM's call: earned respect** (someone shows real respect for what the player said or did; marked *(respect: CHA rep)*) |

- **Coaching (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).** An hour of training with
  a coach working one-on-one (or with a small group) beside the trainee gives the trainee
  **+0.1 extra** in that stat: **+0.2 an hour** in all. It costs both people's time: the
  coach earns their own coaching rep (WIS +0.1 an hour) and does nothing else in that hour.
  The coach should know the thing they're teaching (a rifle shot can coach breathing and
  flinch for a pistol, not knife work).
- **Books (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).** Every book has a **reading
  time** (hours), a **stat** (the one its subject trains: a shooting manual is DEX, a
  smithing or vox manual INT, an interrogation manual CHA), and a **completion bonus**.
  Hours spent reading count as normal training (+0.1 an hour). **Finishing the whole
  book** (all its hours, with notes or practice, not skimming) adds the completion bonus
  to that stat **and half as much again to INT** (reading itself): ordinary manual
  **+1.0 stat, +0.5 INT**; rare or expert **+1.5, +0.75 INT**; exceptional (a Salamander
  forge-book) **+2.0 or more, +1.0 INT or more**. When the subject stat is INT (smithing,
  vox, cogitators), both parts go to INT (+1.5 for an ordinary manual). Some books also
  give a **standing roll bonus** in their subject (e.g. +1 on will checks). Each reader
  gets a book's completion bonus once; rereading gives only the hourly reps. The DM sets
  each book's hours, stat and bonus when it's found; tracked in the campaign's inventory file (Ash on Ostrava: `inventory.md`, Books).
- **Kills count:** +0.1 per kill (**putting an enemy Down counts as a kill**) in the stat it used: a melee kill (blade, axe,
  knife) is STR, a shot or thrown kill (gun, grenade) is DEX.
- **Hours are hours of real work:** a mission day counts what they actually did in
  it; a day in bed gives nothing, unless they study (INT) or coach from the cot (WIS).
- **Specialty days:** a day with at least 3 hours drilling the person's specialty is
  a drill day for it (+1 per 10 drill days, to +5).

**8. Will: interrogation and recruiting.**

Added at the player's request in Ash on Ostrava, session 9. For captives and anyone
the player tries to break or win over.

- **Will (shown as a %, hidden).** When someone is taken, the DM sets their Will
  between **100% and 50%** from what happened before: a crushing defeat, watching
  their people die or a quick surrender start them low; a fanatic, a fresh capture
  or a proud officer starts high.
- **Breaking point (hidden, never told).** Each person also has a hidden number,
  usually **20-50%**, set by the DM from who they are (a fanatic may sit near 0 and
  almost never break). When Will drops **below** it, they break:
  - **Interrogation:** they answer truthfully what they actually know. They can't
    give what they don't know.
  - **Recruiting:** they genuinely want in. They won't betray or fight the player,
    like Nessa. The DM still plays them as themselves.
- **Will checks.** Each push is one session of talk or pressure (about an hour).
  The player says when they're making a will check and rolls a d20; the DM picks the
  skill from the approach (Intimidation, Persuasion, a variant pairing) and takes
  Will down:

  | Total | Will lost |
  | --- | --- |
  | 9 or less | 0-2% |
  | 10-14 | 5-10% |
  | 15-19 | 10-15% |
  | 20+ | 15-20% |

  A natural 20 doubles it. A natural 1 does nothing (no Will lost, set by the player).
- **Hard methods** (pain, threats, deprivation) take **5% more** on each check, but
  roll a d20 each session: **1-3** the subject goes Down (and may die), and anything
  said under pain may be a lie told to make it stop (DM rolls, hidden). Three
  sessions a day at most before they pass out.
- **Who rolls it (Ash on Ostrava, set session 9):** when the player says or does something that would really land, the DM calls the will check and rolls it openly for them; the player doesn't have to ask.
- **Reading them.** A Perception check reads their current Will: **20+** within
  1-2%, **15-19** within 5%, **10-14** within 10%, lower within 20% (or misread).
  The breaking point is never shown.

### Cover (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

Cover adds to the number a shot must reach (the hidden target), for shots from the front or
side it blocks. Shots from behind or above it ignore it.

| Cover | Examples | Shots at you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **None** | open deck, a corridor centre | +0 |
| **Light (1/3)** | thin crates, a cargo net, a makeshift shield, a doorway edge | **+1** |
| **Half** | a ration-crate stack, an overturned table, a low bulkhead, a forged shield | **+2** |
| **Heavy (3/4)** | a hatch frame you lean from, a pillar, a sandbag line, a lifter's chassis | **+4** |
| **Full** | behind a bulkhead or closed hatch, out of sight | **can't be shot**, except by through-cover weapons (anti-materiel rifles), blasts round it (grenades), or flamers |

- **Shooting from cover** costs nothing; **moving** out of it loses it for that beat.
- **Light cover breaks**: two or three heavy hits (or a shotgun at close range) and it's gone.
- **Grenades** and flames ignore cover that doesn't close over the target.
- **Suppressed** targets stay down in their cover (Morcant's -2 applies to their shots).

### Roll checklist (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

Before resolving **any** roll, the DM runs down this list so nothing is missed. Only what
applies is added; each modifier shown in the math.

1. **Base:** d20 + stat bonus (+1 per full 10 levels; **melee: STR, or the better of STR/DEX
   with a finesse weapon such as a knife; house rule 5, "Which stat"**) + proficiency (if trained) + Remarkable
   Athlete / half-proficiency where it applies.
2. **Specialty** (+2, or its drilled level) if the roll is its kind.
3. **Role:** the roller's bestiary role rules, **and any order from a Commander this beat**
   (+2 and acts first; **traitor-elite commanders give two orders a beat to specialists**;
   the Light counts as traitor elites). Leader/rally effects, Morcant's suppression (targets
   he suppresses take -2), jumper/airborne (-2 own shots, -1 for elites; -3 to hit them),
   spotters, bodyguards.
4. **Gear:** attachments by slot (optic or HUD eye, not both), shot-chain modifiers (+2/+1/-1),
   **swapping** (-1), **recoil** (by gun), drums (-1 quick-draw), weapon blessings, crit ranges.
5. **Standing bonuses:** finished books (e.g. +1 will checks), crafting manuals, armour
   (heavy carapace front, Defense style), fields (refractor, conversion).
6. **Conditions:** health step, **food state**, fatigue / no sleep, Hurt shoulder-type limits,
   blinded / dazzled (spec flash), **cover (Cover table: +1 / +2 / +4 / full)**, darkness / smoke, Neglected or Dirty guns (jam range).
7. **Saves:** purity seals (advantage vs warp/plague/madness), Indomitable.
8. **Will checks:** +1 per finished interrogation manual; hard-method +5% and its risk roll.

**Stacking (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).** The **same effect from two places counts once**
(Morcant's suppression -2 is both his role and his specialty: one -2). **Different sources add up** (a leader's
+1 to the side and a Commander's +2 order both apply: +3 on the ordered action).

### A beat (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

A **beat** is one moment of a fight, a few seconds. Everyone acts every beat: **one main thing plus a short move.**

- **Main thing, pick one:** a **shot string** (up to **3 shots** by default, or the gun's own limit if its entry
  gives one: some 6, full-auto 10); a **throw** or one launcher shot; **one melee swing** (below); or something
  else: a patch, a reload, venting, clearing a jam, using an item, a lift-pack hop, a sprint. **A Jumper hops
  and shoots in the same beat** (burst up, hover, fire: one main thing; bestiary roles, Jumper).
- **Melee is one swing a beat.** The attacker swings and the target rolls to defend, all in that beat: it lands,
  is blocked or is parried. **Next beat**, whoever holds the initiative swings: after a block the attacker swings
  again; after a parry the defender does (house rule 5).
- **Short move:** a few metres, or to the next cover, is free. A real sprint is the main thing.
- **Run and gun (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10):** a sprint is still the whole move, but **a gun can still fire a
  shot string during it** (pushing up and shooting), at **-2 on those shots**, the same as firing from the air. A melee swing
  at the end of a sprint still needs Action Surge.
- **Free extras:** a Commander's orders (bestiary roles); a duelist's gun **and** sword in the same beat.
- **Action Surge (Tamsin):** **once per fight**, two main things in one beat. **Extra Attack** is already part of
  his strings; it adds nothing extra.

### Shots and hits (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

What the DM has always done, written down so anyone can run it.

- **To hit:** the DM sets a **hidden target number** for each shot: **10** easy (close, still target),
  **15** normal, **20** hard (far, moving, small). **Cover adds to it** (+1 / +2 / +4; see Cover above).
  The shooter's total (roll checklist) must reach it. Melee between people uses the exchange (house rule 5) instead.
- **Damage:** a hit drops the target **one health step** (Fine → Hurt → Badly hurt → Down),
  **unless their armour stops that kind of weapon** (Part 8, Armour: "shrugs off" means no step for that hit, but **shrugged hits wear them down**: every 3 drop a step, every 5 for power armour).
  Extra health stages (zealots, Skitarii, Ogryns, brutes) add steps before Down.
- **Crit:** drops **one extra step** (crit ranges: a natural 20 for most; Tamsin 19-20; Vigil 17-20 with heat).
- **"Solid hit"** (wording some gear uses): the total **beats the hidden number by 3 or more**.
- **Example:** a cultist behind crates (+2) at normal range: target 17. Tamsin rolls 12 + 7 DEX + 4 proficiency = 23:
  a hit and a solid hit; flak doesn't stop Vigil, so the cultist drops a step.

### Breaking and Down (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

- **An enemy group breaks** when its leader falls or half of it is Down. Then an open d20 (changed by the
  player, Ash on Ostrava session 10): **1-10 they surrender, 11-16 they keep fighting, 17-20 they run.**
  Type shades it: **cultists** rarely surrender, so for them only **1-5 surrenders** (6-16 fight on), unless
  their **leader** has just fallen, when the normal 1-10 applies (bestiary roles, Leader: 'more likely to
  surrender, even fanatics'). Pirates and traitors fall back to better ground rather than give up early.
  (An enforcer still stops breaking while it stands: bestiary roles.) A group that keeps fighting can be
  checked again the next time its losses jump (another quarter Down).
- **Down enemies, after the fight:** d6 each, **1-3 dead, 4-6 alive but out** (prisoners, questioning).
- **The Light (Tamsin and his people) never die from going Down.** A Light-bringer who goes Down is **out of
  the fight**, and **dies only if finished off or hit once more while Down**. **A medic can bring them back:**
  a successful patch (Down → Hurt, one stimm; bestiary roles, Medic) puts them **back in the fight at Hurt** (set
  by the player, session 10, so the medic can save a fight). Unpatched, they sit out the rest of it. Afterwards
  they're out for **more time depending on the hit** (the DM sets it from how bad it was; the medic's and
  surgeon's rolls shorten it).
- **Reps:** putting an enemy Down counts as a kill (house rule 7).

**Playtest mode is on.** Rather than settling every rule up front, we're

## Part 7 — How I Run the Game

My own procedures as DM. Written down so I stay consistent across sessions
instead of drifting.

### Dice are real

*Target numbers stay hidden (Part 6: "Are DCs shown: Hidden, always"); the roll and the modifiers are shown.*

I roll with an actual random number generator, not by choosing a result that
feels right. I show you the roll, the modifier, and the target number. If the
dice say the situation goes badly for you, it goes badly for you.

### Calling for rolls

*The DC is never told before or after the roll (Part 6, house rule 3); the rest of this section stands.*

I only ask for a roll when failure is interesting and success is uncertain. If
there's no meaningful cost to failing, you just succeed. If the action is
impossible, no roll saves it.

When I call for a roll I tell you the DC before you roll, unless the DC is
itself a secret. Default DCs: 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard. I vary them, so if
every check in a session has been DC 15, call me on it.

**Who starts the roll.** I do. The player describes the action in plain
language, and I name the check and the DC. The player never has to know which
skill applies, and is never expected to announce one. "I look around the room"
is the correct input; "I roll Perception" is not required and I don't ask for
it. A player may still propose a check, and I either accept it or name the one
that fits better.

If I don't call for a roll, the action simply works.

**The player reports the raw die, I do the math.** The player rolls the physical
d20 and tells me the number on the face. I apply the ability modifier,
proficiency, and any advantage or situational bonus, and I say what happens.
They are never expected to total their own modifiers.

**Saving throws are told, not blind.** DCs stay hidden, but the threat does not.
I describe what is coming before I call the save: "the vial shatters and green
fog rolls out, give me a Constitution save," never a bare "make a Constitution
save." The player needs to see the threat to decide whether to spend a reaction
or a class feature before the roll lands.

### Pacing and difficulty

The player wants a changing pace: harder when they're cruising, a breather when
they've been beaten down. I deliver that by **changing the challenge, never by
moving the number on a fixed obstacle.**

What I do:

- On a winning streak, the world escalates. The next lock is a masterwork lock,
  not the same lock at a higher DC. The guards are veterans. The ledge is
  genuinely rotten. Very hard and nearly impossible content is on the table, but
  it arrives attached to something that earns it.
- After a rough stretch, the world offers relief. An unlocked side door, an ally
  who owes a favor, a supply cache, a safe place to rest.
- Within the honest range for a given obstacle I use my latitude. A door can
  plausibly be DC 12 or DC 17 depending on what kind of door it is. When the
  player is steamrolling, I pick the heavier door. That is scene design, not
  fudging.

What I don't do:

- Re-price the same obstacle based on how the session is going. If the wall was
  DC 15 an hour ago, it is DC 15 now.
- Raise a DC mid-scene because a roll came in high.

The distinction matters: if the target moves to track the player's streak, their
character sheet stops meaning anything, and the near-miss partial in house rule
1 stops meaning anything with it.

### Describing the world

**Layout is free, hidden things are rolled.** When the player says "paint" or
asks what a place looks like, I describe it without a roll: size, exits, cover,
who is where, anything obvious. A Perception check is only for what is hidden or
easy to miss. When the player enters a new space in a tense moment, I give the
layout as a short list before asking what they do. Detailed written description,
never text maps: the player finds maps confusing. Added at the player's request:
they need to see the space to plan in it.

- I end my turn by handing control back to you, never by deciding what your
  character does, says, or feels.
- I describe what your senses pick up, not what you conclude from it.
- I don't tell you information your character has no way of knowing.
- When you ask "can I…", the answer is yes if it's plausible; the dice sort out
  whether it works.

**No option menus.** I describe the situation and ask "what do you do?" I don't
list choices, offer a numbered menu, or hint at the path I prepared. A menu
shrinks the game to whatever I happened to think of, and the player has asked to
work it out themselves.

The one carve-out is on request. If the player asks what their options are, or
what an action does mechanically, I answer plainly. That's rules knowledge, not
steering. It never comes unprompted.

### Combat

*Superseded by narrative mode (house rule 4): health states, not HP or initiative.*

I track initiative, HP, and conditions for every creature, and I state enemy
HP as descriptions rather than numbers unless you've found a way to know. I
apply monster tactics honestly. Enemies flee, focus fire, and use their abilities.

### Failure states

*Superseded: no HP or death saves. Health states (house rule 4); the Light dies only if finished off or hit again while Down (Part 6, Breaking and Down).*

Death is on the table. When you hit 0 HP you roll death saves like anyone else.
I won't kill you out of nowhere, but I also won't rescue you from a choice you
made with the information available.

### End of session

I write the session log entry, update your character sheet in that campaign's
own file, and tell you what's still unresolved. That way the next session starts
from a real record instead of my memory of it.


**The book.** Each campaign keeps a book file (Ash on Ostrava: `book.md` for Season One, `book-season-2.md` now): the story told as a novel,
third person, past tense. **At the end of every in-game day, right after the daily
tally** (changed by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10: end-of-session writing let
seventeen days pile up unwritten), the DM writes that day as full chapters: every scene, the real choices and
the real words spoken at the table, with the dice invisible and only their
results on the page. Nothing is invented that didn't happen; small connective
detail (weather, a smell, a look) is fine. The campaign sheet carries a short
"Where we stopped" for resuming; the book is the only story file. Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 5.
**Safety net on long days:** after each big scene (a fight, a turning-point talk, a purchase or a
gift), add a one-line note to the campaign sheet's day log, so nothing is lost if the context is
cut mid-day. Those notes are deleted once the day's chapter is written. At the end of a session
the only job left is to check the book is up to date.

### Armour

Armor Class stays off in narrative mode. Armour decides how far down the health
ladder a hit moves you.

**Armour tiers (expanded by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10):**

| Armour | Shrugs off | Still gets through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None, coat | nothing | everything |
| Flak | glancing hits, stub and autogun at range | las, hellguns, shotguns up close, chain, frags |
| **Reinforced flak** | **stub, autogun and glancing las, fully** | clean las hits, hellguns, shotguns up close, chain, frags |
| Carapace | lasguns, autoguns, stub, shotguns at range, most blades, frags (unless the throw was a solid hit) | hellguns, hot-shot, bolters, heavy stubbers, chain, power weapons, plasma, krak |
| **Heavy carapace** | all of the above, **plus hellguns and heavy stubbers at range** | hellguns and heavy stubbers up close (inside ~15 m), bolters, chain on a solid hit, power weapons, plasma, melta, krak |
| **Ceramite-forged** | all of the above, **plus hellguns and heavy stubbers even up close** | bolters, chain, power weapons, plasma, melta, krak |
| Power armour | almost everything a soldier carries | bolters, plasma, melta, power weapons, krak |

- **Part-armour:** a plate that only covers one side counts only from that side (Tamsin's heavy front: shots
  from the side or back hit plain carapace; a chest-only carapace protects the chest).
- **Crits always get through.** Against armour that would have shrugged the hit, a crit drops just one step (no extra).
- **Wear-down: shrugged hits still hurt.** Armour stops a hit from dropping a step, not from mattering. **Every 3
  shrugged hits on the same person in one fight drop them one step** (power armour: every 5). Enough hellgun
  shots will hurt even a man in power armour.

Armour takes damage and the DM says when it is failing.

### Weapons

No weapon has a damage number. Better weapons drop a target in fewer hits and
get through better armour. Ammunition is the real cost: hot-shot packs, bolt

the campaign sheet's Full kit lists each one as *Name (base) + attachments*.

### The hub

Each 40k campaign has a home base, like Darktide's Mourningstar, where missions
start and end. It holds the people who upgrade, heal, bless and brief the
player. Between missions, time at the hub is a short or long rest as the fiction
allows.

### Training at the hub

*Superseded by house rule 7 (training reps) in Ash on Ostrava.* Once per stay at the hub, the player may train one thing: shooting, melee,
fitness. One roll. On a success, the character carries a **sharp edge** into the
next mission: advantage once on a roll of that kind, then it is spent. A
natural 20 also earns something in the fiction (respect, a trick, a rival). A
failure costs nothing but time; a natural 1 means a minor strain, nothing that
carries into the mission.

### The quartermaster's stock

The hub's armoury stock **refreshes every 2 days** (Shop size by market, below): a handful of items,
priced in salvage or trade, never coin. Some are canon, some invented for the
setting. **Rolled stock (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 8).** Stock is never
picked to match the wish list. It's rolled at random from the 40k gear pools in
[`wh40k_gear/`](wh40k_gear) (~260 items: weapons, armour, mods, gear, augmetics,
salvage, vehicle parts, goods) with `python3 wh40k_gear/roll.py SELLER SIZE`.
Each seller type rolls from its own pool (Munitorum, black market, Mechanicus,
Free Trader, Ecclesiarchy); a small hub rolls 5-7 items, a big one 14-18;
common, uncommon and rare items are weighted; and one open d20 on a natural 20
adds an exotic oddity (xenos, archeotech). The DM doesn't read the pools to the
player, so finds stay surprises. Enemy loot still follows the fiction, and the DM
may still invent gear. The player may ask the quartermaster to **hold** an item; the
quartermaster decides in character whether to, and for how long.

**The quartermaster's slate (account).** Set in Ash on Ostrava, session 7. The
quartermaster keeps a running account for the player in TV. Selling is done by
word: the player names what in the hub's storage to sell, the quartermaster
prices it, and his ratings fetch it, so nothing has to be carried by hand.
The value goes onto the slate, and purchases (his stock or Void Exchange orders)
come off it. The balance lives in the campaign sheet. Imperial Thrones exist as
coin in the setting, but the table counts everything in TV. **Rate (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 9): 100 Thrones = 1 TV**
(Dark Heresy scale: a lasgun ~75 Thrones, an autogun ~100, a night's lodging ~5, a
manufactorum worker's month ~30). A brief 5 = 1 rate covered one purchase at Voss's
stores (session 9); that purchase stands.

**Coin on a body:** see Part 8, Loot.


### Renown (set by the player, session 9)

How far the player character's name has spread. **When he walks into a new place
(a station, a district, a bar, a hive level), roll d20 + Renown bonus: 15+ someone
recognises him.** Then the DM rolls who: friend, admirer, rival, or enemy (a cultist, a
bounty hunter, someone he wronged). Where a deed happened, add **+5 there**.

| Renown | Who knows him | Bonus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown | Nobody | +0 (only a natural 20) |
| Local | One station or fleet | +3 |
| Regional | A few systems | +6 |
| Sector | The whole sector | +10 |
| Legend | Worlds away | +15 (rarely needs a roll) |

Renown rises by the DM's call after big, public deeds (a cult broken, a world saved, a
famous enemy killed); it is tracked on the campaign sheet.

### Power rating (reference only; set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10)

**A number for looking at, nothing more. It has no effect on any roll, fight, price or
outcome.** It shows at a glance how dangerous someone is, on one scale for everyone:
**0 = a regular civilian, ~100 = a Poor-wealth cultist**, open-ended upward.

**Power rating = Body + Skill + Weapons + Armour + Kit**

| Part | How it scores |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | STR + DEX + CON levels, minus 45 (a civilian is about 15 each, so 0) |
| **Skill** | Civilian 0 · cultist 30 · ganger, raider or pirate 40 · Guard or traitor soldier 50 · veteran or officer 60-70 · storm trooper or Scion-trained 80 · player character **10 × level**. Plus **+20** for a specialist role (or the Commander role), **+10** per specialty (a trainee's young focus +5) |
| **Weapons** | Best weapon: scrap blade 20 · scrap gun 35 · common gun or blade 50 · hellgun-class 70 · heavy or special (bolter, plasma, chainsword, power weapon, launcher, heavy stubber) 90 · exotic 120. **+15** masterwork, blessed or a true custom build · **+10** per extra weapon (up to +30) · **+5 per fitted attachment** on any carried weapon, one per slot (up to +30; repairs and internals don't count) |
| **Armour** | None 0 · rags or coat 5 · flak 20 · reinforced flak 25 · chest-only carapace 30 · carapace 40 · heavy carapace (or a heavy front) 50 · ceramite-forged 60 · power armour 100. **+10** shield · **+20** refractor · **+30** conversion field |
| **Kit** | **+5** per grenade type carried (up to 20) · augmetic eye 5-25 (shooting eye 20, sensor-type optics 15) · lift or jump pack 15 · auspex 10 · stimms or drag-harness medic kit 5 |

### Nemeses: the DM's call (changed by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10; first set session 9, after Shadow of Mordor)

**No automatic survivor rolls.** After a fight, the DM writes down in the hidden notes **who got away or was
left alive and saw what happened**: where it was, what they saw Tamsin do, and how they feel about it
("two dockers saw him shoot the gardener at the Mart; scared, and angry"). From then on it's the DM's call:
when the story fits, **one of them comes back** (in a fight, a deal, a bar), **recognises Tamsin, and calls
him out**, carrying the grudge, often risen (better gear, followers, a new paymaster) with a mark from how
they survived (a scar, a burn, an augmetic, a fear, a vow). The DM may roll an open d20 when it's genuinely
undecided whether one turns up. **Hidden:** the player learns who they are only when they come back. It
ends when they're killed, captured or turned. Nemeses made under the old rule stay on the list.

### Crew posts (set by the player, session 9)

Support people who stay aboard the ship (or at a base) and keep the Light running. A
**staffed post** gives a standing bonus. A Light-bringer may hold a post while not on
a mission (Dace as smith); otherwise a crew member is hired or recruited for it.

- **Cook:** keeps the Light fed. **Healing rolls while resting aboard get +2**, and the ship's meals count as **Well fed** (house rules, Food).
- **Smith / armourer:** maintains the Light's weapons and armour. **Each fight, the
  first jam, misfire or gear quirk is ignored**; field damage is mended between
  missions at no cost; does the crafting rolls for builds.
- **Ship's surgeon:** a specialist, good at nothing else (no missions, useless in a
  fight). **+4 on all treatment and surgery rolls; anyone recovering aboard heals in
  half the time** (stacks with the cook); removes lodged rounds and shrapnel, does
  what a field patch can't, **fits augmetics** without a station medicae; rolls to
  pull a Down patient through. One surgery at a time. (The field medic stays a combat
  role, like Sallow.)
- **Armsman:** ship security. Guards the cells, the hold and the ship while the
  Light is out; the first line if anyone boards.
- **Vox-officer:** runs the ship's comms from aboard: keeps the Light's channels
  encrypted and re-keyed, watches them for intruders, relays to the team. (The field
  vox/auspex role, like Brenner, stays with the team.)
- **Enginseer:** keeps the ship's engines and systems running and the machine
  spirits content. (Ferro-Ashe holds it unofficially: cast out by the Mechanicus, so
  the work is real but the title isn't.)
- **Navigator:** a warp-eyed mutant of a noble Navigator house who steers the ship
  through the warp: **faster and safer jumps.** Very rare; a ship this size seldom
  rates one.
- **Astropath:** a psyker who sends messages across the stars, so the Light can reach
  its Inquisitor without borrowing a fleet's. **Super rare: only assigned to special
  people, by the Ordo's grant.**
- **Crewman / deckhand:** ship upkeep, loading and hauling. Loot and kit are hauled
  aboard and stowed without the Light carrying it; **the ship is ready to leave in
  half the usual time** after a mission.
- **Chaplain:** morale and faith. **+2 on the Light's saves against fear and the
  warp**; can bless gear with purity seals (codex, Purity seals).
- **Quartermaster:** keeps the container and the stores. **Tallies the haul after
  each mission and suggests what to keep, sell or turn in; keeps tabs on everyone's
  kit and resupplies what was used**, and **cleans the guns people hand in** (set by the player, session 10) (Tavi's spent krak grenades come back out of
  stock, or go on the buy list). **Goes to the shops, calls the player with what they
  have, and buys what the player picks**, haggling by their CHA: **d20 + CHA bonus: 9
  or less full price, 10-14 10% off, 15-19 20% off, 20+ 30% off** (and sells loot for
  the same % more). One run per shop restock.

### Shop size by market (set by the player, session 9)

A shop's stock size follows how big and rich its market is, not just the counter:
**small** (5-7 items: a stall, a frontier post, a ship's locker), **medium** (9-12: a
town, a small station, a chapel), **large** (14-18: a city district, a big station's
traders), **huge** (20-25: a fleet anchorage's Munitorum stores, a hive market, a trade
hub like the Mart). Roll with `python3 wh40k_gear/roll.py SELLER SIZE`. A big place has
**several shops**, one per seller type, each rolled separately. **Shops restock every 2
days** (stock holds for two days, then rolls fresh).

### The Void Exchange

A wider, dearer market beside the quartermaster, added at the player's request
(Ash on Ostrava, session 5), like Darktide's Brunt's Armoury. In Ash on Ostrava
it is **Mag Vashti's Void Exchange**, a Free Trader Ottrick buys from wholesale.

- **Range:** almost anything, including rare and odd items.
- **Price:** about double the quartermaster's for the same item. Trade goods
  only (salvage, weapons, relics); no favours.
- **When:** only in real space, never in the warp. Orders go by vox or astropath.
- **Delivery:** days. A courier meets the ship or it waits at the next port.
- **Risk:** the DM rolls on arrival; a delivery can be late, damaged or not
  quite what was ordered.

The quartermaster also takes **favours** as payment: a small favour (an errand,
a watch, a bet, an extra item from a drop) buys one small item.

### The mission terminal

Set by the player for Ash on Ostrava, Season Two; usable in any 40k campaign. Like
Darktide's mission board. At the hub (or aboard the player's ship), the player
describes the next mission as if briefing the squad: target, objective, what they
expect to find. The DM answers out of character with:

- **Difficulty:** Easy, Standard, Hard, Brutal or Nightmare, judged honestly from
  the target and the squad's strength. Missions are not meant to be easy.
- **Tweaks:** anything the DM would change or add to make it fit the setting
  (a complication, a modifier, a rumour of what's really there).
- **Reward guide:** the kind of loot and salvage the mission can yield.

**Target lists (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 8).** The patron may load
a short list of targets (confirmed or rumoured, rolled openly) as a starter. The
player may pick one, change it, or make up their own mission instead; the DM
judges any of them the same way.

Then the mission is played. Harder missions mean tougher enemies and better loot,
never a moved DC (Part 7).

### Gear rules (apply to every weapon)

These rules apply across many items. Each item's own rules are in the gear
catalogue: read its table of contents,
[`wh40k_gear/catalogue/README.md`](wh40k_gear/catalogue/README.md), and open only
the entry that comes up in play.

**Hot-shot conversion** (set session 8). Any las weapon can take a hot-shot
conversion kit: it **hits one step harder** (a lasgun hits like a hellgun), **uses
ammunition twice as fast**, and a gun that builds heat gains **1 extra heat per

**Drop-prone guns (set by the player, session 8).** A gun with no safety, a hair or
light trigger, or a cocked hammer is **drop-prone** (the Kasr Sprinter; the DM says
which others are, case by case). When one is dropped, or its bearer falls, is
knocked down or crash-lands with it, roll a d20:

| d20 | The gun |
| --- | --- |
| 1-8 | **fires** (roll again below) |
| 9-16 | is **damaged**: out of the fight until repaired |
| 17-20 | is fine |

If it fires, roll a d20 for where the shot goes: **1-8 a friendly**, **9-15 a wall or
the floor**, **16-20 an enemy**. Guns built never to fire by accident (the Nocturne
Ember) are exempt.

**Going Down drops your weapons.** When anyone (the Light or an enemy) goes
**Down**, whatever is in their hands falls. A long gun **on a sling** stays on them
and doesn't count as dropped. A gun with no sling, or a pistol in hand, hits the
deck: it can be grabbed or kicked away, and a drop-prone one rolls on the table
above. Holstered and sheathed weapons stay put.

**Plasma heat.** Plasma weapons build heat: **standard plasma, 1 heat per 2 shots
fired in a fight.** At **3 heat** the weapon must vent for one beat. Firing
anyway at 3 heat: d20, on 1-5 it **overheats** (the bearer is burned, Hurt, and
the weapon is out of the fight). Heat clears after venting or when the fight
ends. Slow-filled and rushed cells keep their own misfire chances.

**Ammo crates.** Whenever ammunition turns up, in a shop roll or as
loot, roll a d20: **on 15+, or when the story makes it plain (an armoury, a supply
dump), it's an ammo crate: four times the amount.** In shops a crate costs 3.5x
the single price.

**Shields.** A shield takes one hand: only a pistol or a one-handed melee weapon
in the other. **In melee** its bonus goes on the defender's roll in the exchange
(a raw 17+ still can't be blocked). **Against shots** it is cover the bearer
carries, from the front only; shots from the side or behind ignore it. Going Down
drops it, like anything in hand. Each shield's numbers are in the catalogue (kit).

**Airborne (jump packs and lift packs; set by the player, session 9).** While someone
is in the air on a pack: **their own shots take -2** (traitor elites: -1); **shots at
them take -3**; their enemies may leave cover to get a shot at them; and anyone who
shoots at an airborne jumper this beat is **open to surprise** from the ground (+2 on
attacks against them, and they can't react to it). Each pack's lift, charge and quirks
are in the catalogue (kit). The **Jumper** role is in the bestiary (roles).

**Swapping, recoil and attachment slots (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 10).**
**Swapping:** a shot at a *different* target than your last shot in the same string takes
**-1**. **Recoil:** every gun has a rating; each shot in a string **after the first** takes
**-recoil**: **0** las weapons (lasguns, hellguns, laspistols, hellpistols) and stub/auto
pistols; **1** autoguns on burst, SMGs and machine pistols, shotguns, revolvers, hand cannons;
**2** bolt weapons, heavy stubbers, anti-materiel rifles. **Slots:** a gun takes **one
attachment per slot**: muzzle, under-barrel, stock, optic, grip, magazine, trigger, barrel.
No hard cap on bonuses; every attachment carries a real downside. **A gun's optic and a HUD
eye's aim bonus don't stack** on the same shot: use the better. Attachments are in the
catalogue (kit: pistol and gun attachments).

**Gun condition and jams (set by the player, session 9).** Every gun is **Clean**,
**Dirty** or **Neglected**. **Clean** (cleaned after its last fight, or within ~3 days):
never jams; a natural 1 is just a miss. **Dirty** (**about 30 shots since its last cleaning**,
changed by the player, session 10; or ~3 days of use): **a natural 1 jams it**; clearing takes a beat.
**Neglected** (a week or more uncleaned, or exposed to mud, grit, vacuum or a gale):
**jams on 1-3; a natural 1 seizes it**, out of the fight until a smith fixes it. **Las
guns count one step cleaner.** Never-jam guns (the 'Kasr Frontier') ignore all this,
and the smith crew post ignores the first jam each fight. **Cleaning:** ~30 minutes a
gun (DEX training, +0.1 an hour). **Enemies:** cultists and raiders roll **Dirty or
Neglected** (even chance); pirates **Dirty**; traitors and traitor elites **Clean**.
The roller prints it. The Light's gun condition is tracked on the campaign sheet (one line under the Full kit:
only the exceptions are written). **Aboard ship the Light's guns are cleaned routinely, mostly by the
quartermaster: people hand their guns in to him and he cleans them.**

**Refractor field (set by the player, session 9; reworked session 10).** A worn field
generator. **Light hits** (anything carapace would shrug off: stub, autogun, las, shotgun
at range, shrapnel, ordinary blades) are **stopped automatically**; the air shimmers.
**Heavy hits** (anything that gets through carapace: hellguns, bolters, heavy stubbers,
power, chain and plasma weapons): **roll a d6, 5-6 the field takes it.** **Overload:** it
stops at most **3 light hits in one beat**; a 4th or more that beat get through and the
field is **down for 1 beat** while it resets. Assassinorum shield-breaker rounds go
straight through.

**Conversion field (set by the player, session 9).** A stronger field that turns the
energy of a hit into light. **When a hit lands on the wearer, roll a d6: on 4-6 the
field takes it, with a blinding flash** (an attacker within ~5 m takes -2 on their next
shot). **On 1-3 the shot goes through and the field burns out: it needs a repair**
(a tech-adept such as Ferro-Ashe, about an hour) before it works again. A **damaged**
conversion field blocks only on 5-6.

**Doses: stimms and combat drugs.** When you take a dose, roll a d6: **1-3 it
lasts 3 turns, 4-6 it lasts 4 turns.** When those turns are over you can take
another dose to keep it going (roll again). **One turn after the last dose ends,
you crash: drop to Badly hurt.** Chain **3 or more doses** in a row and the crash
puts you **straight to Down** instead. A combat drug that rolls 18-20 on its
backfire doesn't crash.

### Perils of the Warp

**The Perils trigger.** Any psyker (enemy or the Light) rolls a d20 **once per
power, when it fires** (a charge is still just one roll). The range that triggers
Perils is set by the item or role (a witch's staff 1-3, a rogue psyker 1-4, a cult
psyker 1-6).

**The Perils result.** When the trigger hits, roll a d20 on this table:

| d20 | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Daemon breach.** The psyker is torn apart and a random warp daemon takes their place |
| 2-4 | **Warp blast.** The psyker goes Down; everyone within ~3 m drops a health step |
| 5-8 | **Backlash.** The power fails and a force blast throws the psyker away, into or out of the fight: they drop a health step and lose their next turn |
| 9-12 | **Burn.** The power fails; the psyker drops a health step |
| 13-16 | **Misfire.** The power hits a random target nearby, friend or enemy; the psyker takes -2 next beat |
| 17-19 | **Nosebleed.** The power fails; no powers next beat |
| 20 | **Overload.** The power fires at double strength; the psyker drops a health step |

### Psyker powers

A rogue psyker without a staff knows **2 powers**: roll a d10 twice (reroll a 10
or a repeat). Witches with a staff keep the staff's list and god powers. **Charge**
is how many beats the power builds before it fires (0 = fires this beat).
Wounding, stunning or disarming the psyker breaks a charge, as with the staff.

| d10 | Charge | Power | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 beat | **Lightning** | Hits one target and one person beside them. Ignores flak; anyone hit takes -2 next beat |
| 2 | 1 beat | **Force blast** | Hits like a hellgun and knocks the target down |
| 3 | 0 | **Warp ward** | Blocks the first shot and the first melee hit at them this beat (a raw 17+ still gets through) |
| 4 | 2 beats | **Mind-lash** | One target: WIS save or frozen a turn (a purity seal gives advantage) |
| 5 | 2 beats | **Telekinesis** | Rips a weapon from a hand, or pulls one person 4 m (out of cover, off a ledge) |
| 6 | 2 beats | **Warp-fire** | Hits like a flamer and sets the target alight |
| 7 | 3 beats | **Soulblaze ball** | Launches a fiery ball that drifts slowly to where it's sent, stops, then explodes: everything within 10 m catches fire |
| 8 | 3 beats | **Power force charge** | Moves cover, objects and people away from or towards the psyker. Throwing someone into a wall drops them a health step; can rip weapons apart |
| 9 | 2 beats | **Great warp shield** | A bigger warp ward that covers 2-3 people close by. Each beat it's up, roll the Perils trigger, and its range grows by 1 (1-4, then 1-5, then 1-6...) |

### Custom weapons and crafting

Set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 8. Two ways to make your own gear:

1. **Add it to the pool (out of character).** The player describes an item (what
   it is, what it does, how rare it should feel). The DM sets a price and rarity
   that fit the setting and adds it to `wh40k_gear/build_pools.py`, so it can turn
   up in shops and loot. If it has rules of its own, its entry goes in the right
   file under `wh40k_gear/catalogue/` with a line in that folder's README (the
   table of contents); only rules that apply to many items go in Gear rules here. Same fairness test as blessings: accept, adjust, or decline with the
   reason.
2. **Build it in the story (in character).** A crafter in the party or at the
   hub builds it:
   - **Design:** the player describes the weapon; the DM judges fairness.
   - **Cost:** a base weapon or frame plus salvage (plasteel, machine-cores,
     ceramite, parts) named by the DM, taken from stock.
   - **Fractions (set by the player, Ash on Ostrava session 9):** salvage counts in
     quarters. A small part uses **1/4** or **1/2** of an item (an ingot, a core, a
     plate); the rest stays in stock and is counted as 1/4, 1/2 or 3/4.
   - **Time:** days to weeks, by complexity.
   - **The roll:** the crafter rolls openly, with their specialty bonus.
     **Success:** works as designed. **Near miss (1-2 under):** works, with a
     quirk (runs hot, jams sometimes, heavier). **Fail:** some parts wasted; may
     try again. **Natural 20:** masterwork, better than designed, perhaps with a
     spirit of its own.

### Purity seals

A priest blesses gear and stamps a seal on it. Each seal gives **advantage on
one save** against warp or Chaos corruption (plague, possession, madness) and
then burns away. Free from a priest who judges the soldier worthy. Faith in this
setting is sometimes literally armour.

**Earned seals.** One working seal per weapon (or armour) gives the mechanic.
Extra seals are honours: when the player does something worthy of it, the hub's
priest summons them unprompted and seals a piece of their gear, chosen by the
DM. Honour seals have no mechanical effect; they record deeds. Risk earns them,
asking does not.

### Weapon blessings

The Darktide system, adapted. A tech-priest consecrates a weapon to the
Omnissiah and gives it **one blessing**: a small, specific edge on that weapon
only. Blessings cost **salvage** (Mechanicus parts, plasteel, rare metals)
brought back from missions, never money. A weapon holds one blessing; replacing
it destroys the old one. Examples:

| Blessing | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Rending teeth (chain weapon) | Raw 16 or higher is unstoppable in the exchange, instead of 17 |
| Steady machine-spirit (gun) | Once per mission, reroll one attack with this weapon |
| Efficient coils (energy weapon) | Ammunition lasts noticeably longer |
| Bloodthirsty spirit (melee) | A parry with this weapon lets you strike before initiative passes |

Salvage is shared demand: the quartermaster wants it too.

The player may propose their own blessings. It has to fit the weapon and the
fiction, and be about as strong as the examples. The DM decides whether it is
fair, and may accept it, adjust it, or turn it down with the reason.

## Quick Reference Card

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| How do I do the thing? | d20 + modifier + proficiency vs. DC |
| Advantage and disadvantage together? | They cancel; roll one d20 |
| Crit damage? | Double the dice, not the modifier |
| I'm at 0 HP? | d20 each turn, 10+ succeeds, 3 successes stable, 3 failures dead |
| Concentration save? | CON save, DC 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher |
| Can I cast two spells in a turn? | Only if one is a bonus-action spell and the other is a cantrip |
| Can I move and attack? | Yes, and you can split movement around the attack |
| Do I get a bonus action? | Only if something specifically gives you one |
| Does a nat 1 fail a skill check? | No. Nat 20 and nat 1 are automatic only on attacks and death saves |
| I missed the DC by 1? | House rule 1: partial success on ability checks, with a cost |
